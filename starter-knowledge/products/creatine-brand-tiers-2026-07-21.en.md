# Creatine: Specific Brand and Product Tiers

::: warning Keep Two Things Separate
Creatine's **T1** means there is relatively mature human evidence for improvements in strength and lean mass when combined with resistance training. The **P1–P3** grades below cover only labeling, certification, and transparency for specific products. P1 does not mean stronger effects.
:::

## 30-Second Conclusion

- Differences between creatine products are much smaller than differences between fish oils: the core ingredient is creatine monohydrate, and the key issues are **purity and label accuracy**;
- First tier: **Thorne Creatine** and **Momentous Creatine Monohydrate**, both with specific products verifiable in the NSF database;
- Second tier: **NOW Foods**, **Life Extension**, and **Optimum Nutrition**, with GMP or Informed Sport signals but no independent product-level batch report;
- Third tier: **BulkSupplements** and similar products, which claim third-party testing but lack verifiable product-level certification;
- There is no need to pursue HCl, buffered, ethyl ester, or other "new" forms: nearly all research uses monohydrate.

## Grading Rationale

### P1: Thorne and Momentous

Both specific product names and forms can be verified in NSF's official dietary supplement certification directory:

- **Thorne Creatine**: the NSF directory lists "Thorne® Creatine, All Powder, 1 scoop (5 g)";
- **Momentous Creatine Monohydrate**: the NSF directory lists "Creatine Monohydrate, All Powder, 1 scoop (5 g)"; vegetarian and chewable versions also exist.

NSF certification means the product has undergone content verification, contaminant testing, and label review. NSF's focus is not identical to IFOS (there is no equivalent creatine-specific certification), and the batch in hand still needs checking.

### P2: NOW, Life Extension, and Optimum Nutrition

- **NOW Foods**: a large GMP-certified manufacturer with a broad product line and low prices. Its **Sports-line creatine** (including the 8 oz/227 g package sold on iHerb) carries Informed Sport certification, meaning batches are tested for prohibited substances. This is a stronger product-level signal than GMP alone, but it does not equal NSF-style comprehensive content and contaminant verification, so it remains P2;
- **Life Extension**: relatively transparent brand, but no independent certification for its creatine product was found in this round;
- **Optimum Nutrition**: Informed Sport certified, primarily for prohibited-substance testing; its 3.4 g serving is below the common 5 g serving.

### P3: BulkSupplements

Very low cost and a third-party-testing claim, but independent product-level certification remains unverified. This does not mean the product is necessarily poor; it means the publicly verifiable information does not support a higher tier yet.

## Category-Specific Risks for Creatine Products

Creatine has fewer product-quality risks than fish oil, but the following still matter:

1. **Purity**: low-quality creatine may contain synthetic byproducts such as dicyandiamide and dihydrotriazine;
2. **Label accuracy**: whether the actual creatine content per serving matches the label;
3. **Heavy metals**: raw-material sources and manufacturing can introduce lead, arsenic, or other contaminants;
4. **Confusion between forms**: HCl, ethyl ester, buffered, and other "new" forms provide less creatine by weight than monohydrate and lack comparable independent research;
5. **Micronization**: a physical treatment that improves dissolution speed but does not change efficacy.

## Purchase Checklist

1. The label says **Creatine Monohydrate**, not another form;
2. 5 g per serving, or adjusted to match the study dose;
3. Keep the ingredient list short; BCAAs, caffeine, and other combinations are unnecessary without a clear reason;
4. Powder generally offers better value than capsules;
5. Buy through an authorized channel.

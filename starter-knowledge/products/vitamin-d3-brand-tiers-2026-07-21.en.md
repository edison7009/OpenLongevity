# Vitamin D3: Specific Brand and Product Tiers

::: warning Keep Two Things Separate
Vitamin D3's **T2** means it can be valuable in deficiency or specific populations. The **P1–P3** grades cover only labeling, certification, and transparency for specific products.
:::

## 30-Second Conclusion

- Vitamin D3 is an established nutrient, and product differences mainly involve **certification, dosage form, and whether K2 is included**;
- First tier: **Thorne Vitamin D/K2 Liquid** and **Momentous Vitamin D3**, both NSF certified;
- Second tier: **NOW Foods** (GMP and very low cost), **Life Extension**, and **Sports Research**;
- A D3+K2 combination is not essential; evidence that K2 protects blood vessels remains insufficient;
- **Test 25(OH)D before deciding whether supplementation is needed and at what dose**.

## Grading Rationale

### P1: Thorne and Momentous

NSF's official dietary supplement certification directory (checked 2026-07-21) lists:

**Thorne:**
- Vitamin D + K2, Liquid, 6 drops
- Vitamin D, Liquid, 6 drops
- Vitamin D/K2 Liquid (Canada), Liquid, 1 drop

**Momentous:**
- Vitamin D3 2000IU, Capsule, 1 capsule
- Vitamin D, Capsule, 1 capsule

### P2: NOW, Life Extension, Sports Research, Nordic Naturals, and Pure Encapsulations

- **NOW Foods**: a large GMP manufacturer with very low prices but no independent product-level certification. For an established nutrient, GMP-level quality control may be adequate for routine use. Its D3+K2 capsule (D3 1000 IU + K2 MK-4 45 μg) has a moderate dose suitable as a maintenance starting point for people without a blood test. Note that the K2 is MK-4, unlike the MK-7 used in many D3+K2 products; MK-4 has a shorter half-life and requires consistent daily intake;
- **Sports Research**: D3+K2 combination with a coconut-oil base to support fat-soluble absorption, but the 5000 IU dose is relatively high;
- **Nordic Naturals**: strong in fish oil, while D3 is not its core product line.

## Category-Specific Risks for D3 Products

1. **D3 vs D2**: prefer D3 (cholecalciferol), which generally raises 25(OH)D more and maintains it longer;
2. **Dose misuse**: 5000 IU is unsuitable for long-term use without testing; the NIH tolerable upper intake level is 4000 IU/day;
3. **D3+K2 combinations**: evidence that K2 protects against vascular calcification remains insufficient (NIH ODS), so the combination is not required;
4. **Fat solubility**: absorption is better when taken with a meal containing fat;
5. **Drug interactions**: check thiazide diuretics, glucocorticoids, orlistat, and others;
6. **Hypercalcemia risk**: excessive intake can cause hypercalcemia, kidney stones, and soft-tissue calcification.

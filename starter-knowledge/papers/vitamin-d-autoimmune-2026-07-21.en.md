# Vitamin D and Autoimmune Disease Evidence Batch

## One-Sentence Conclusion

The five-year randomized phase of VITAL confirmed a 22% reduction in the composite autoimmune-disease outcome (HR 0.61 after excluding the first two years). Mechanistic research supports biological plausibility; specific dose regimens for people with a family history fall within medical decision-making.

## vd-vital-autoimmune-2022

- Type: prespecified ancillary study of a large randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 2×2 factorial trial;
- Population: 25,871 US adults, men ≥50 years and women ≥55 years, mean age 67.1 years; not selected for family history of autoimmunity, autoantibody positivity, or vitamin D deficiency;
- Intervention: cholecalciferol D3 2000 IU/day versus placebo; concurrently randomized to Omega-3 1 g/day or placebo;
- Duration: median 5.3 years;
- Primary result: 123 versus 155 confirmed autoimmune-disease cases, adjusted HR 0.78 (95% CI 0.61–0.99, P=0.05); crude absolute incidence was approximately 0.95% versus 1.20%;
- Lag analysis: after excluding events in the first two years, HR 0.61 (0.43–0.86, P=0.005), but the linear interaction P for change in vitamin D effect over time was 0.14 and the nonlinear interaction P was 0.34;
- Other results: HR 0.85 (0.70–1.02, P=0.09) for confirmed plus probable cases; no individual disease difference was significant; the signal was stronger in the lower-BMI subgroup. The positive family-history interaction applied to Omega-3, not vitamin D;
- Limitations: composite outcome and ancillary endpoint; dedicated study is needed for dose regimens in high-risk people with a family history;
- Identifiers: [PMID 35082139](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35082139/), [PMCID PMC8791065](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8791065/), DOI `10.1136/bmj-2021-066452`.

## vd-vital-autoimmune-followup-2024

- Type: two-year observational extension after the randomized VITAL trial ended;
- Population: 21,592 participants continued, representing 83.5% of the original randomized population;
- Exposure: analyzed by original randomized group, although randomized pills had stopped and participants could supplement independently;
- Primary result: after approximately seven total years, 255 versus 259 confirmed autoimmune-disease cases; HR 0.98 (95% CI 0.83–1.17) in the original vitamin D randomization group;
- After updating randomized-phase cases: the randomized-phase vitamin D HR changed from the originally reported 0.78 to 0.85 (0.70–1.04);
- Contamination: during observation, 28.3% reported independent vitamin D use >800 IU/day; HR after censoring these participants was 0.95 (0.78–1.15);
- Interpretation: cumulative post-discontinuation analysis was affected by independent supplementation by 28.3% of participants; individual responses after discontinuation vary;
- Identifiers: [PMID 38272846](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38272846/), [PMCID PMC11565399](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11565399/), DOI `10.1002/art.42811`.

## vd-treg-review-2019

- Type: systematic review of eight randomized controlled trials;
- Scope: seven autoimmune-disease trials and one healthy-participant trial, assessing Treg number, proportion, or function;
- Results: among five trials measuring Treg proportions in autoimmune disease, four found higher proportions in the vitamin D group at 12 months; in the healthy-participant trial, values were 6.4% versus 5.5% at 12 weeks;
- Limitations: studies were highly heterogeneous; Treg is a mechanistic marker and should be interpreted alongside clinical-outcome trials;
- Identifiers: [PMID 31550254](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31550254/), [PMCID PMC6759203](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6759203/), DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0222313`.

## vd-treg-t1d-2015

- Type: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled exploratory trial;
- Population: 30 young patients with newly diagnosed type 1 diabetes;
- Intervention: D3 70 IU/kg/day or placebo for 12 months;
- Results: ex vivo suppressive capacity of Tregs increased from baseline in the D3 group, and the between-group difference in change at 12 months was statistically significant;
- Limitations: very small sample, participants already had disease, and the endpoint was ex vivo immune function; applicable to a diseased population and assessed separately for healthy people. The “37.2% versus 0.7%” stated in a video is not the correct between-group change reported in the abstract; the original abstract reports 22.2±47.2% versus −16.6±21.1%;
- Identifiers: [PMID 26277548](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26277548/), DOI `10.1016/j.clim.2015.08.002`.

## vd-incident-autoimmune-meta-2025

- Type: systematic review and meta-analysis of 18 controlled studies;
- Primary result: overall RR 0.99 (95% CI 0.81–1.20) for incident autoimmune disease with vitamin D;
- Limitations: included studies, diseases, and doses were heterogeneous; dose-subgroup findings should not be treated as causal dose-response evidence;
- Role: provides a perspective across doses and populations for interpreting the VITAL signal;
- Identifiers: [PMID 39717776](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39717776/), DOI `10.3389/fimmu.2024.1453703`.

## Research Interpretation

1. `22%` is a relative-risk reduction; the absolute difference is approximately `0.25` percentage points. Both should be displayed.
2. `39%` comes from the prespecified analysis excluding the first two years, but the time interaction was not significant and should not be called a confirmed “golden window.”
3. Treg, VDR, Th1/Th17, and tolerogenic dendritic-cell research explains mechanisms of vitamin D immune modulation.
4. Family history, high BMI, autoantibody positivity, and an established autoimmune diagnosis are distinct clinical questions and should be assessed separately.

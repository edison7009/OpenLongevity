## cr-2017

- Item: creatine;
- Type: meta-analysis;
- Title: Effect of creatine supplementation during resistance training on lean tissue mass and muscular strength in older adults: a meta-analysis;
- Journal: Open Access Journal of Sports Medicine;
- Population: 22 studies, 721 participants, mean age approximately 57–70 years;
- Duration: 7–52 weeks, training 2–3 times per week;
- Results: compared with placebo plus training, the creatine group gained an average of 1.37 kg more lean mass; bench-press and leg-press strength showed small but significant advantages;
- Limitations: heterogeneous protocols, doses, and populations; primarily supports benefit “in combination with training”;
- Source: [PMID 29138605](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29138605/).

## cr-2024-function

- Item: creatine;
- Type: systematic review and meta-analysis;
- Title: Creatine supplementation for optimization of physical function in the patient at risk of functional disability;
- Journal: JPEN;
- Population: 33 RCTs, 1,076 participants;
- Scope: older adults and adults with chronic disease;
- Results: sit-to-stand performance SMD 0.51; small improvements in upper-body strength and grip strength; mean lean-mass increase of approximately 1.08 kg;
- Limitations: evidence quality for most outcomes was low or very low;
- Source: [PMID 38417175](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38417175/).

## cr-2024-cognition

- Item: creatine;
- Type: systematic review and meta-analysis;
- Title: The effects of creatine supplementation on cognitive function in adults;
- Journal: Frontiers in Nutrition;
- Population: randomized controlled trials in adults;
- Search range: 1993–2024;
- Results: positive signals for memory and some processing-speed measures;
- Limitations: findings for global cognition, executive function, attention, and other domains were inconsistent, with low certainty;
- Source: [PMID 39070254](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39070254/).

## om-2024-events

- Item: Omega-3;
- Type: meta-analysis;
- Title: Effects of omega-3 fatty acids on coronary revascularization and cardiovascular events;
- Journal: European Journal of Preventive Cardiology;
- Population: 18 RCTs, 134,144 participants;
- Duration: 4.5 months to 7.4 years;
- Results: revascularization RR 0.90, myocardial infarction RR 0.89, and cardiovascular mortality RR 0.92; overall effects were modest, with a stronger signal for EPA-only formulations;
- Limitations: formulations and populations were highly heterogeneous, with I² of 68% for revascularization; the research department had previously received unrestricted funding from relevant manufacturers;
- Source: [PMID 38869144](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38869144/).

## om-2024-rx

- Item: Omega-3;
- Type: meta-analysis;
- Title: The effect of omega-3 Polyunsaturated Fatty Acid prescription preparations on the prevention of clinical cardiovascular disease;
- Journal: Nutrition Journal;
- Population: 12 RCTs, 99,830 participants;
- Duration: at least one year in every study;
- Results: cardiovascular effects varied with composition, dose, and participants’ risk profile;
- Limitations: only prescription formulations were included, so findings cannot be directly extrapolated to retail fish oil;
- Source: [PMID 39639295](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39639295/).

## q10-2026

- Item: coenzyme Q10;
- Type: RCT;
- Title: Coenzyme Q10 supplementation raises plasma levels without improving mitochondrial function in older adults;
- Journal: GeroScience;
- Population: 40 community-dwelling older adults, mean age 74 years;
- Intervention: 400 mg/day for 12 weeks;
- Results: plasma Q10 increased, but muscle mitochondrial function, VO₂max, glucose metabolism, and body composition did not improve;
- Limitations: small sample, short duration, and relatively healthy participants; does not answer questions about specific disease populations;
- Source: [PMID 41604113](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41604113/).

## q10-2022

- Item: coenzyme Q10;
- Type: meta-analysis;
- Title: Effects of Coenzyme Q10 supplementation on biomarkers of oxidative stress in adults;
- Journal: Antioxidants;
- Population: randomized controlled trials in adults;
- Results: oxidative-stress markers decreased in some disease populations, with no consistent effect in healthy subgroups;
- Limitations: biomarkers are not healthspan outcomes; diseases, doses, and trial quality varied substantially;
- Source: [PMC9311997](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9311997/).

## nmn-2025

- Item: NMN and NR;
- Type: systematic review and meta-analysis;
- Title: The Effect of Nicotinamide Mononucleotide and Riboside on Skeletal Muscle Mass and Function;
- Journal: Journal of Cachexia, Sarcopenia and Muscle;
- Population: RCTs with mean ages of 60.9–83 years;
- Results: NMN did not significantly improve skeletal-muscle index, grip strength, walking speed, or five-times sit-to-stand performance;
- Limitations: trials and sample sizes were limited, but current evidence does not support its use to preserve muscle mass or function in older adults;
- Source: [PMID 40275690](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40275690/).

## nmn-2024

- Item: NMN;
- Type: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial;
- Title: Ingestion of β-nicotinamide mononucleotide increased blood NAD levels, maintained walking speed, and improved sleep quality in older adults;
- Journal: GeroScience;
- Population: 60 older adults;
- Duration: 12 weeks;
- Results: blood NAD-related markers increased, with positive signals for walking speed and subjective sleep;
- Limitations: small sample and short duration; several authors were affiliated with product-related companies;
- Source: [PMID 38789831](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38789831/).

## nr-2023-mci

- Item: NR;
- Type: randomized, placebo-controlled pilot trial;
- Title: A randomized placebo-controlled trial of nicotinamide riboside in older adults with mild cognitive impairment;
- Population: 20 older adults with mild cognitive impairment;
- Intervention: titrated to 1 g/day for 10 weeks;
- Results: blood NAD⁺ increased to approximately 2.6 times baseline, with no between-group difference in reported adverse events; MoCA and other cognitive and psychological measures did not improve;
- Limitations: extremely small sample; cerebral blood flow and methylation clocks were exploratory and unstable after multiple-comparison adjustment; material was supplied by ChromaDex, and a coauthor holds NR-related intellectual property and serves as the company’s chief scientific adviser;
- Source: [PMID 37994989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37994989/).

## nr-2025-bp

- Item: NR;
- Type: randomized, placebo-controlled pilot trial;
- Title: Nicotinamide riboside combined with exercise to treat hypertension in middle-aged and older adults;
- Population: 54 sedentary adults aged over 55 years with hypertension;
- Intervention: NR 1 g/day or placebo, both combined with six weeks of exercise;
- Results: NR plus exercise was not superior to placebo plus exercise for daytime systolic blood pressure;
- Limitations: small sample, short duration, and both groups exercised; does not address longer-term use or other populations;
- Source: [PMID 40770531](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40770531/).

## nad-2025-human-review

- Item: NAD⁺ metabolism and precursors;
- Type: review of human clinical evidence;
- Title: NAD⁺ precursor supplementation in human ageing: clinical evidence and challenges;
- Results: evidence for age-related decline across human tissues remains limited; precursor trials affect biochemical markers, but clinical efficacy is limited;
- Limitations: the review describes the overall research landscape but cannot replace systematic assessment of each intervention;
- Source: [PMID 41083806](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41083806/).

## nad-2026-wellness-review

- Item: NAD⁺, NR, NMN, and injectable routes;
- Type: PRISMA-guided systematic review;
- Title: NAD⁺ supplementation for anti-aging and wellness;
- Scope: 33 human interventions and 80 rodent studies;
- Results: NR/NMN consistently increased circulating NAD-related markers; human functional, metabolic, vascular, and healthspan-related outcomes were heterogeneous and frequently null;
- Limitations: no eligible intravenous or intramuscular NAD⁺ anti-aging/wellness outcome trials were found;
- Source: [PMID 41655607](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41655607/).

## nad-2026-iv-tolerability

- Item: intravenous NAD⁺ and intravenous NR;
- Type: retrospective pilot study using electronic health records in a commercial setting;
- Intervention: 500 mg intravenous infusions on four consecutive days, with 30 days of follow-up;
- Results: during infusion, the NAD⁺ group reported moderate-to-severe gastrointestinal symptoms, increased heart rate, and chest pressure; mean infusion time was 97 minutes;
- Limitations: nonrandomized commercial setting with limited sample and outcomes; exploratory metabolic markers;
- Source: [PMID 41704678](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41704678/).

## nad-2025-heart-failure

- Item: intravenous NAD⁺;
- Type: single-center randomized placebo-controlled trial;
- Population: 180 patients with heart failure due to ischemic cardiomyopathy, LVEF ≤45%, NYHA II–III;
- Intervention: NAD⁺ 10 mg/day or placebo, both with guideline-directed therapy, for seven days;
- Results: one-month LVEF was 45.44% versus 42.44%, P=.024; six-month composite events, heart-failure hospitalization, and NYHA improvement showed trends only and did not reach statistical significance;
- Limitations: specific disease, single center, and short treatment course; applies to this disease context;
- Source: [PMID 40954388](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40954388/).

## sp-2022

- Item: spermidine;
- Type: RCT;
- Title: Effects of Spermidine Supplementation on Cognition and Biomarkers in Older Adults With Subjective Cognitive Decline;
- Journal: JAMA Network Open;
- Population: 100 participants, mean age 69 years;
- Intervention: 0.9 mg/day for 12 months;
- Results: between-group difference in the primary memory endpoint −0.03, 95% CI −0.11 to 0.05, P=.47;
- Limitations: exploratory verbal-memory and inflammatory signals require new confirmatory trials;
- Source: [PMID 35616942](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35616942/).

## sp-2024

- Item: spermidine;
- Type: RCT;
- Title: Supplementation of spermidine at 40 mg/day has minimal effects on circulating polyamines;
- Journal: Nutrition Research;
- Population: 37 healthy men aged 50–70 years;
- Intervention: 40 mg/day for 28 days;
- Results: short-term tolerability was acceptable, but changes in serum and urinary polyamines were small;
- Limitations: focused on short-term safety and exposure rather than clinical benefit; men only and very short duration;
- Source: [PMID 39405978](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39405978/).

## sp-2026-immune

- Item: spermidine;
- Type: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled pilot trial;
- Title: Spermidine Mitigates Immune Cell Senescence and Boosts Vaccine Responses in Healthy Older Adults;
- Population: 40 adults aged over 65 years after a third SARS-CoV-2 vaccine dose;
- Intervention: 6 mg/day for 13 weeks;
- Results: among participants with an initially low vaccine response, some antibody, memory B-cell, and immunosenescence markers improved;
- Limitations: specific context, small sample, and mainly surrogate endpoints; several authors were advisers to related supplement companies and the trial included company funding;
- Source: [PMID 42169618](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42169618/).

## ergo-2024

- Item: ergothioneine;
- Type: exploratory RCT;
- Title: Investigating the efficacy of ergothioneine to delay cognitive decline in mild cognitively impaired subjects;
- Journal: Journal of Alzheimer's Disease;
- Population: 19 adults aged over 60 years with mild cognitive impairment;
- Intervention: 25 mg three times per week for one year;
- Results: positive signals for learning ability and neurofilament light chain;
- Limitations: a 19-person exploratory trial that provides a basis for larger trials;
- Source: [PMID 39544014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39544014/).

## ergo-2025

- Item: ergothioneine;
- Type: review;
- Title: Ergothioneine for cognitive health, longevity and healthy ageing: where are we now?;
- Journal: Proceedings of the Nutrition Society;
- Scope: mechanistic, observational, and early intervention research;
- Results: directions involving cognition, sleep, and neurodegeneration markers warrant further testing;
- Limitations: most conclusions rely on observational data or small interventions;
- Source: [PMID 40968729](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40968729/).

## pqq-2022

- Item: PQQ;
- Type: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial;
- Title: Effect of Dietary Pyrroloquinoline Quinone Disodium Salt on Cognitive Function in Healthy Volunteers;
- Population: 64 healthy Japanese adults aged 40–79 years, 58 completing the study;
- Intervention: PQQ disodium salt 21.5 mg/day for 12 weeks;
- Results: the study reported improvements over placebo across multiple Cognitrax cognitive domains, DECO, and MMSE-J;
- Limitations: small sample, multiple outcomes, and short duration; used a specific commercial ingredient and included authors affiliated with its manufacturer;
- Source: [PMID 34415830](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34415830/).

## pqq-2024

- Item: PQQ and hydrogen combination;
- Type: RCT;
- Title: The impact of six-week dihydrogen-pyrroloquinoline quinone supplementation on mitochondrial biomarkers, brain metabolism, and cognition;
- Journal: Journal of Nutrition, Health & Aging;
- Population: adults over 65 years with mild cognitive impairment;
- Duration: six weeks;
- Results: the between-group interaction for MMSE was nonsignificant, while isolated orientation and brain-metabolism measures showed positive signals;
- Limitations: PQQ and hydrogen were used together, so the effect of PQQ alone cannot be identified;
- Source: [PMID 38908296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38908296/).

## pqq-2024-review

- Item: PQQ;
- Type: review of human studies;
- Title: The effects of pyrroloquinoline quinone on human health;
- Journal: Journal of Medical Investigation;
- Scope: multiple small, short-term human studies;
- Results: scattered positive findings for sleep, fatigue, cognition, and inflammatory markers;
- Limitations: small trials, many outcomes, prominent combination-product and conflict-of-interest issues, and no hard clinical outcomes;
- Source: [J-STAGE PDF](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmi/71/1.2/71_23/_pdf).

## ca-akg-2020-mouse

- Item: Ca-AKG;
- Type: animal experiment;
- Title: Alpha-Ketoglutarate, an Endogenous Metabolite, Extends Lifespan and Compresses Morbidity in Aging Mice;
- Results: mice showed increased lifespan and compressed late-life morbidity;
- Limitations: animal research supports progression to human trials only and cannot establish human dose, efficacy, or safety;
- Source: [PMID 32877690](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32877690/).

## ca-akg-2021

- Item: Ca-AKG combination;
- Type: retrospective uncontrolled before-after comparison;
- Title: Rejuvant®, a potential life-extending compound formulation with alpha-ketoglutarate and vitamins;
- Population: 42 participants, mean use approximately seven months;
- Results: the TruAge DNA methylation clock decreased by an average of approximately eight years;
- Limitations: no randomization or placebo, combination product, and inability to exclude regression to the mean or lifestyle confounding; commercial relationships exist, and the result cannot be interpreted as actual rejuvenation or life extension;
- Source: [PMID 34847066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34847066/).

## ca-akg-2023-protocol

- Item: Ca-AKG;
- Type: randomized-trial protocol;
- Title: Alpha-ketoglutarate supplementation and BiologicaL agE in middle-aged adults (ABLE);
- Design: 120 adults aged 40–60 years whose methylation age exceeded chronological age; sustained-release Ca-AKG 1 g/day versus placebo for six months;
- Primary outcome: change in DNA methylation age;
- Limitations: this is a protocol paper, not an efficacy result; the primary endpoint remains a surrogate;
- Sources: [PMID 37217632](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37217632/), [NCT05706389](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05706389).

## ca-akg-2025-feasibility

- Item: Ca-AKG;
- Type: ABLE recruitment-feasibility analysis;
- Results: 467 people expressed interest and 120 were ultimately recruited, demonstrating that screening participants by methylation age was feasible in this study;
- Limitations: Ca-AKG efficacy was not reported and this cannot be described as a positive RCT;
- Source: [PMID 40819772](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40819772/).

## ca-akg-2026-cohort

- Item: Ca-AKG;
- Type: cross-sectional cohort;
- Population: 4,260 health enthusiasts who purchased a salivary methylation test and completed a questionnaire;
- Results: use of a sustained-release Ca-AKG plus vitamin product was associated with an average 1.8-year lower age residual; the association with ordinary AKG was smaller and nonsignificant, and the longitudinal subset was nonsignificant after multivariable adjustment;
- Limitations: proprietary 9-CpG clock, cross-sectional design, healthy-user bias, and recruitment bias;
- Source: [PMID 42166733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42166733/).

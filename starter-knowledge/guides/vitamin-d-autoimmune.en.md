# Vitamin D and Autoimmune Disease

::: tip One-sentence conclusion
The randomized VITAL phase observed a reduction in the composite autoimmune-disease outcome from about 1.20% to 0.95%, but the difference was not maintained in the post-randomization extension. This is a signal worth tracking, not a basis for recommending high doses to everyone.
:::

## Key Data

| Phase | Population and intervention | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Randomized VITAL phase | 25,871 older US adults, D3 2000 IU/day, median 5.3 years | 123 vs 155 cases; HR 0.78 (95% CI 0.61–0.99) | About 22% relative reduction and 0.25 percentage-point absolute difference |
| Post-discontinuation extension | 21,592 continued follow-up and could self-supplement | About seven-year cumulative HR 0.98 (0.83–1.17) | Benefit did not persist; 28.3% self-supplementation created contamination |
| 2025 meta-analysis | 18 controlled studies | Incident autoimmune disease RR 0.99 (0.81–1.20) | High heterogeneity across diseases, doses, and populations |

## Practical Meaning

- Family history, vitamin D deficiency, autoantibody positivity, and established autoimmune disease are different questions;
- Review 25(OH)D, diet, sun exposure, medication, and disease context before discussing dose;
- Treg and immune-pathway findings support mechanistic plausibility but cannot replace clinical outcomes;
- High-dose use in autoimmune disease is a medical decision.

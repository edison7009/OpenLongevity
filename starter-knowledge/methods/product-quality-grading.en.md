# Supplement Product Quality Grading

Status: `working-standard`  
Version: `0.1`  
Date: `2026-07-20`

## Two Completely Different Axes

| Axis | Question answered | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence Tier (T1–T4) | How much human evidence exists for this intervention, for whom, and for which outcomes? | DHA/EPA is currently T1, but applicability depends on the specific goal |
| Product Tier (P1–P3) | Is this particular product accurate, pure, fresh, and traceable? | Life Extension **Super Omega-3** is currently rated P1 |

A P1 product does not turn a T3 intervention into T1; a T1 intervention also does not mean every product with the same name is worth buying.

## Why Brands Alone Cannot Be Ranked

The same company may sell products with different doses, source materials, chemical forms, and entirely different certification status. The grading unit must therefore be:

```text
Brand + full product name + market/version + label date + lot (if available)
```

“Thorne is top tier” can only serve as shorthand. The actual conclusion should be: “The current US version of Thorne Super EPA entered P1 in this review.”

## Grading Dimensions

### 1. Product identity

- Official page, full name, SKU/UPC, and market version;
- Currently on sale;
- Number of units per serving and active ingredients are clear on the label;
- A certificate from another product by the same brand must not be transferred to this one.

### 2. Content and form

- Record active-ingredient content rather than total raw-material weight;
- Distinguish per-unit from per-serving amounts;
- Distinguish single-ingredient from combination products;
- Record chemical form; when undisclosed, write “undisclosed” rather than infer it from a marketing name.

### 3. Independent quality evidence

Order of priority:

1. The specific product and current lot can be verified in a certifying body’s database;
2. A public COA containing the lot number, laboratory, date, methods, and results;
3. Independent certification for the specific product without public lot-level results;
4. A manufacturer claim of “every lot tested”;
5. GMP, raw-material supplier, or general brand-quality statements only.

GMP primarily describes the manufacturing system. It does not mean the actual content, freshness, and contaminants of that bottle have been publicly verified.

### 4. Category-specific risks

For fish oil/algal oil, check at least:

- EPA and DHA label compliance;
- Oxidation measures such as peroxide value, anisidine value, or TOTOX;
- Mercury, lead, cadmium, and arsenic;
- Persistent pollutants such as PCBs and dioxins;
- Packaging, antioxidant measures, storage, and transport;
- Raw-material origin, traceability, and sustainability.

A third-party report that tests only heavy metals does not mean oxidation has also been tested.

### 5. Public information and commercial relationships

- Public-use records involving celebrities, physicians, or Bryan are labeled separately;
- Personal protocols, sponsorships, affiliate links, brand partnerships, and second-hand reports must be distinguished;
- Celebrity use does not raise a product’s quality grade or establish efficacy;
- Paid reviews, consumer scores, and sales volume are excluded from quality grading.

## Product Tiers

### P1: Relatively complete product-level evidence

- The current, specific product identity is clear;
- Active ingredients and per-serving amounts are transparent;
- Independently verifiable product-level certification or a reasonably complete lot report is available;
- Key category-specific risks are substantively covered;
- No major information gap affects the purchase decision.

For P1, the lot number, source, and storage condition of the bottle in hand must still be checked.

### P2: Generally credible, with transparency gaps

- Product and dose are clear;
- Reasonable manufacturing, ingredient, or testing information is available;
- However, a current public lot report, oxidation data, complete contaminant data, or independent database verification is missing;
- Suitable as a candidate, but should not be described as a “buy without hesitation.”

### P3: Relies mainly on manufacturer claims

- The current product exists and its label is broadly clear;
- Verifiable independent product-level information is lacking;
- Information about key forms, oxidation, freshness, or contaminants is limited;
- This does not mean the product is necessarily poor; it only means the publicly verifiable information has not reached a higher tier.

### pending / unverified

- `pending`: not yet reviewed;
- `unverified`: product identity, market version, or certificate identity cannot be matched reliably;
- After discontinuation, reformulation, or certificate expiry, the old grade automatically becomes `stale` and cannot be carried forward.

## Final Pre-purchase Check

Even when a page assigns P1, confirm at purchase:

1. The full product name and SKU match;
2. The seller is official or authorized;
3. The lot in hand corresponds to the report;
4. Expiry date and transport temperature;
5. Actual active ingredients per serving;
6. Allergens, combination ingredients, and drug interactions;
7. The product form matches the research goal.

In the United States, dietary supplements are not reviewed by the FDA for safety and efficacy before marketing in the same way as drugs. Therefore, “FDA registered facility” or “made in an FDA inspected facility” cannot substitute for product-quality evidence.

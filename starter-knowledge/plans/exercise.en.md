# Training Plan

::: tip Choose frequency before the split
Choose by the number of days you can train consistently, not by assuming that more divisions are more advanced. Beginners and people with two or three available days should usually start with full-body training. For most people who can reliably train four days, an upper/lower split is the most balanced option.
:::

## Choose a Training Split

| Option | Suggested frequency | Best suited to | Guidance |
|---|---:|---|---|
| [Full body (1-day split)](plans/training/01-full-body.en.md) | 2–3 days/week | Beginners, returning trainees, limited time | Beginner first choice |
| [Upper/lower (2-day split)](plans/training/02-upper-lower.en.md) | 4 days/week | Most consistent trainees | **Best overall** |
| [Push/pull/legs (3-day split)](plans/training/03-push-pull-legs.en.md) | 3 or 6 days/week | Experienced with exercises, enjoys a gym rhythm | Higher recovery demand |
| [Chest/back/legs/shoulders and arms (4-day split)](plans/training/04-body-part.en.md) | 4–5 days/week | Hypertrophy focus, prefers body-part sessions | Advanced preference |

## General Execution Rules

- Keep roughly two or three repetitions in reserve for most working sets; every set does not need to reach failure.
- Add a small amount of load after reaching the top of the repetition range with stable technique in two consecutive sessions.
- Rest two to three minutes for compound lifts and 60–90 seconds for isolation work.
- Stop and seek qualified assessment for chest pain, fainting, acute injury, or unusual shortness of breath.

> A split is only a way to distribute weekly training. Long-term progress depends more on consistency, total training volume, movement quality, progressive overload, and recovery.

# Chest/Back/Legs/Shoulders and Arms (4-Day Split)

| Item | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Best suited to | Experienced trainees focused on hypertrophy who prefer concentrating on fewer body parts per session |
| Frequency | Four sessions per week; small additional sets can provide a second exposure for a priority area |
| Duration | About 60–80 minutes per session |
| Advantage | More local training time and easy placement of accessory exercises |

::: warning Four divisions do not automatically mean more advanced
If one day is often missed, that body part may receive no training for the whole week. Full-body or upper/lower training is usually easier to sustain for most people training primarily for health.
:::

## Four-Day Template

| Day | Main exercises | Suggested volume |
|---|---|---|
| Chest and triceps | Bench press, incline press, chest fly, triceps pressdown | Three compound sets; two or three accessory sets |
| Back and biceps | Pull-up or pulldown, row, rear deltoid work, biceps curl | Three compound sets; two or three accessory sets |
| Legs | Squat or leg press, Romanian deadlift, split squat, leg curl, calves | Two or three sets each |
| Shoulders and arms | Overhead press, lateral raise, rear deltoid work, biceps, triceps | Two or three sets each |

## Weekly Schedule

- Example: chest Monday, back Tuesday, legs Thursday, and shoulders and arms Saturday.
- Do not begin with very high per-session volume for one body part. Start around 6–10 weekly sets and observe recovery.
- When longevity and function are primary goals, retain loaded carries, core stability, and weekly aerobic training.

# Push/Pull/Legs (3-Day Split)

| Item | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Best suited to | Intermediate trainees familiar with basic exercises who enjoy a gym-based rhythm |
| Frequency | Rotate across three weekly sessions; six weekly sessions create much greater recovery demand |
| Duration | About 55–80 minutes per session |
| Advantage | Similar movement patterns are grouped together, making exercise selection intuitive |

::: warning Six days is not the default recommendation
The six-day version is only appropriate when sleep, time, and recovery are consistently strong. Most people do not need to force a higher frequency just to follow a conventional PPL schedule.
:::

## Three-Day Exercise Template

| Day | Main exercises | Suggested volume |
|---|---|---|
| Push | Bench press, overhead press, incline press, lateral raise, triceps pressdown | Two or three sets each |
| Pull | Pull-up or pulldown, row, rear deltoid work, biceps curl, farmer carry | Two or three sets each |
| Legs | Squat or leg press, Romanian deadlift, split squat, leg curl, calves, core | Two or three sets each |

## Weekly Schedule

- Three days: push Monday, pull Wednesday, and legs Friday; rotate to pull, legs, and push in the following week.
- Six days: push/pull/legs/push/pull/legs/rest; use fewer sets per session than in the three-day version.
- If performance declines for two consecutive weeks, sleep worsens, or joints become irritated, reduce volume or add rest days first.

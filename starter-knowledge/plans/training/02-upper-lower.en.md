# Upper/Lower (2-Day Split)

| Item | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Best suited to | Most trainees with a foundation who can consistently train four days per week |
| Frequency | Four sessions per week, training each major area about twice |
| Duration | About 55–75 minutes per session |
| Advantage | A balanced tradeoff among frequency, per-session volume, and recovery |

::: tip Best overall for most consistent trainees
If only three sessions are usually completed, full-body training is often better than an upper/lower split with frequently missed days.
:::

## Four-Day Template

| Day | Main exercises | Suggested volume |
|---|---|---|
| Upper A | Bench press, row, overhead press, lat pulldown, triceps | Three sets for primary lifts; two or three for the rest |
| Lower A | Squat, Romanian deadlift, lunge, calves, core | Three sets for primary lifts; two or three for the rest |
| Upper B | Incline press, pull-up or pulldown, machine row, lateral raise, biceps | Two or three sets each |
| Lower B | Deadlift variation or hip thrust, leg press, leg curl, split squat, loaded carry | Two or three sets each |

## Weekly Schedule

- Upper A Monday, Lower A Tuesday, Upper B Thursday, and Lower B Saturday.
- Use mostly 5–8 reps for primary lifts and 8–15 for other work, keeping two or three repetitions in reserve.
- When lower-body recovery is slow, reduce accessory work before replacing the whole split.

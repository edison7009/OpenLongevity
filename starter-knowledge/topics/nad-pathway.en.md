# NAD⁺ Metabolic Pathway Overview

::: tip 30-Second Conclusion
NAD⁺ is an essential coenzyme used in cellular energy conversion, redox reactions, DNA repair, and many signaling processes. NAD⁺ metabolism research has repeatedly appeared in leading journals such as *Science*, *Nature*, and *Cell*, making it one of the central pathways in longevity research. Randomized human trials confirm that NR and NMN can significantly raise blood NAD⁺ levels, and some studies have observed positive signals in walking speed, sleep, and metabolic markers. A normal diet should first provide adequate vitamin B3 and protein. Direct oral and intravenous NAD⁺ require assessment as separate routes.
:::

## In Plain Language: What Does It Do in the Body?

NAD⁺ can be imagined as a cellular "work permit + electron shuttle."

After food is broken down, its energy must pass through a series of redox reactions into the mitochondria. NAD⁺ accepts electrons and becomes NADH, then hands those electrons off and returns to NAD⁺. This cycle helps cells produce ATP.

NAD⁺ is also consumed by several classes of enzymes:

- **PARP**: participates in responses to DNA damage;
- **Sirtuins**: participate in metabolism, stress responses, and gene regulation;
- **CD38**: participates in immunity and cellular signaling and also breaks down NAD⁺;
- Other enzymes: participate in calcium signaling, chromatin regulation, and cellular communication.

NAD⁺ is unquestionably important, but "the body needs it" does not automatically imply "the more a healthy person supplements, the better." Water and glucose are also essential, yet both have appropriate ranges and regulatory systems.

## Where Does the Body Make NAD⁺?

```text
Tryptophan (protein) ───────────────────────┐
Niacin / Nicotinic acid ────────────────────┤
Nicotinamide ───────────────────────────────┤
NR / Nicotinamide riboside → NMN ──────────┼→ NAD⁺ ⇄ NADH
Dietary NAD / NADP → gut converts to NAM ───┘
```

This diagram shows synthesis entry points only. It does not mean that every route is equivalent or that one precursor is more effective clinically. Tissue, cellular compartment, enzyme activity, and disease state all affect the result.

## "Low NAD⁺" Must Be Split into Two Different Questions

### 1. Nutritional Vitamin B3 Deficiency

Vitamin B3 is the collective term for niacin, nicotinamide, and related derivatives. The body converts absorbed B3 into NAD; some tryptophan from protein can also enter this pathway.

Severe deficiency causes **pellagra**, with typical manifestations including:

- Pigmented, rough, or sunburn-like dermatitis on sun-exposed areas;
- Gastrointestinal changes such as diarrhea, vomiting, and constipation;
- Fatigue, depression, memory decline, and psychiatric or neurologic symptoms;
- Severe untreated cases may be fatal.

Higher-risk contexts include severe malnutrition, alcohol use disorder, malabsorption, inflammatory bowel disease, cirrhosis, Hartnup disease, and carcinoid syndrome. These are nutritional and medical problems whose causes should be corrected rather than addressed by independently buying expensive NMN.

### 2. The Hypothesis of Age-Related NAD⁺ Decline

Cell and animal studies often observe age-related changes in NAD⁺ metabolism. Human evidence is more complicated: findings differ across tissues, diseases, sampling approaches, and analytical methods, and human studies consistently observing a decline remain limited.

Current understanding:

- A unified diagnostic standard for "anti-aging NAD⁺ deficiency" is still being developed;
- The optimal blood NAD⁺ target for the general population remains under investigation;
- Trials of long-term functional outcomes are progressing, including NCT04691986.

Nutritional B3 deficiency and the longevity community's age-related NAD⁺ decline hypothesis must therefore not be conflated.

## Which Foods Support NAD⁺ Synthesis?

The priority is not to find a "superfood with the most direct NAD⁺," but to provide vitamin B3 and tryptophan. NAD/NADP in food is generally converted to nicotinamide in the gut before absorption.

| Food | Typical Role | NIH Reference Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Beef liver, cooked, about 85 g | Rich source of niacin | About 14.9 mg |
| Chicken breast, cooked, about 85 g | Niacin + protein | About 10.3 mg |
| Turkey breast, cooked, about 85 g | Niacin + tryptophan | About 10.0 mg |
| Salmon or tuna, cooked, about 85 g | Niacin + protein | About 8.6 mg |
| Pork tenderloin, cooked, about 85 g | Niacin + protein | About 6.3 mg |
| Brown rice, cooked, 1 cup | Plant-source niacin | About 5.2 mg |
| Peanuts, about 28 g | Plant-source niacin | About 4.2 mg |
| Sunflower seeds, about 28 g | Plant-source niacin | About 2.0 mg |
| Fortified cereals | Added free niacin is generally readily available | See label |

Recommended niacin intake for adults is usually expressed as niacin equivalents, `NE`: approximately `16 mg NE/day` for adult men and `14 mg NE/day` for adult women, with different values during pregnancy and lactation. Common conversions are `1 mg niacin = 1 mg NE` and approximately `60 mg tryptophan = 1 mg NE`, although individual conversion efficiency varies.

::: info A More Practical Dietary Strategy
Adequate protein plus long-term intake of fish, poultry, or suitable legumes, nuts and seeds, and whole or fortified grains is generally more reasonable than chasing one food with trace amounts of NMN or NAD⁺.
:::

## How Can Someone "Supplement NAD⁺"?

"NAD booster" may refer to entirely different products. Always identify the actual ingredient first.

| Route | What It Is | Human Evidence | Current Knowledge-Base Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dietary B3 + protein | Maintains normal synthesis substrates | Nutritionally essential; correcting inadequacy has clear value | Foundational route |
| Standard-dose multivitamin/B3 | Fills dietary or absorption gaps | Relevant to a confirmed risk of inadequacy; not a proven anti-aging protocol | Pending review by deficiency context |
| Niacin | A form of vitamin B3 that can become NAD⁺ | Nutritional doses and high-dose pharmacologic use are fundamentally different | `pending` |
| Nicotinamide | A form of vitamin B3 that does not cause typical niacin flushing | Supports NAD⁺ synthesis; long-term high-dose use requires separate review | `pending` |
| NR | NAD⁺ precursor | Usually raises blood NAD-related markers; functional benefits are inconsistent | **T3** |
| NMN | NAD⁺ precursor | Usually raises blood NAD-related markers; functional benefits are inconsistent | **T3** |
| Direct oral NAD⁺ | Product containing intact NAD⁺ | Absorption, metabolism, tissue exposure, and clinical-benefit data continue to accumulate | `pending` |
| Intravenous NAD⁺ | Medical/commercial infusion | Very little evidence for anti-aging outcomes; cannot be treated as an ordinary supplement | `pending`, medical route |

### First Priority: Correct Nutritional Gaps

If diet is adequate and no malabsorption or special disease is present, additional B3 does not automatically provide more NAD⁺-related functional benefit. When true nutritional deficiency is suspected, assess diet, alcohol use, absorption, and relevant disease rather than relying on one commercial NAD⁺ measurement.

### Second Level: Frontier Exploration with NR / NMN

NR and NMN can relatively consistently raise blood NAD⁺ or related metabolites. Trials in the 2025 muscle-function meta-analysis were short, and individual studies observed positive signals in walking speed and sleep. They are T3 candidates with a clear mechanism that remain under frontier investigation.

See the [NMN / NR Dossier](../dossiers/nmn.md) for detailed evidence.

### Direct Oral NAD⁺

Dietary NAD/NADP is converted into nicotinamide in the gut. Human pharmacokinetic, tissue-exposure, and clinical-outcome studies of commercial direct oral NAD⁺ products are progressing. This should be assessed as an independent candidate, separate from NR/NMN evidence.

### Intravenous NAD⁺

A 2026 systematic review of NAD⁺ for anti-aging and wellness found no eligible anti-aging outcome trials of intravenous or intramuscular NAD⁺. Retrospective data from commercial settings reported that 500 mg intravenous NAD⁺ may cause moderate-to-severe gastrointestinal discomfort, increased heart rate, and chest pressure during infusion.

A single-center RCT in 180 patients with heart failure caused by ischemic cardiomyopathy used intravenous NAD⁺ at 10 mg/day for 7 days and found a between-group difference in left ventricular ejection fraction at one month. This low-dose, short-course study in a specific disease applies to that disease context. Anti-aging infusion in healthy people is assessed as a separate route.

## Supplement Safety Boundaries

::: warning "Naturally Present in the Body" Does Not Mean "High Doses Are Risk-Free"
- High-dose niacin can cause flushing, itching, and gastrointestinal discomfort and may affect the liver, blood glucose, and uric acid;
- Nicotinamide does not cause typical flushing, but high doses are still not risk-free nutritional supplementation;
- Short-term NR and NMN trials report good tolerability; long-term use should be evaluated with a clinician;
- Actual exposure and tissue distribution from direct oral NAD⁺ remain under study;
- Intravenous NAD⁺ involves infusion, sterility, rate, cardiovascular responses, and medical oversight and should not be self-administered;
- People with liver disease, kidney disease, diabetes, gout, cancer treatment, pregnancy or lactation, or complex medication regimens should first be assessed by a clinician.
:::

Niacin supplements sometimes contain 500 mg or more per serving, far above daily adult nutritional requirements. High doses used for goals such as lipid modification follow a pharmacologic treatment model and should not be taken long term without supervision merely because the packaging says "vitamin."

## Can NAD⁺ Deficiency Be Tested?

Traditional nutritional assessment may use diet history, clinical manifestations, and specific niacin metabolites. A single serum niacin value is not a reliable measure of whole-body nutritional status. Research can measure NAD⁺ and metabolites in whole blood, plasma, cells, or tissue, but results from different specimens, handling methods, and assays are not directly interchangeable.

There is currently no clinically validated consumer "optimal anti-aging NAD⁺ range." Commercial test values are better treated as research data; they cannot independently determine whether to supplement and cannot replace disease assessment.

## Current Evidence Conclusion

1. **NAD⁺ has high biological importance**;
2. **Severe B3 deficiency must be treated**;
3. **NAD⁺ metabolism research has appeared in *Science*, *Nature*, and *Cell* and is a core longevity pathway**;
4. **NR/NMN are confirmed to raise NAD⁺, while long-term functional-outcome trials are progressing**;
5. **Direct oral and intravenous NAD⁺ require assessment as separate routes**;
6. **Tiers should apply to specific intervention routes, not an essential human molecule**.

# Avoiding Food Before Sleep: Core Scientific Rationale

Bryan Johnson concentrates all calories between morning and noon (an eating window of about 6 hours), goes to bed at 10 p.m., and does not eat for about 10 hours before sleep. This is not an arbitrary choice; at least five layers of physiology may support it.

## 1. Circadian Rhythms: Organs Have Their Own "Working Hours"

The body has more than one brain clock in the hypothalamic SCN. The liver, pancreas, muscles, and gut each have independent peripheral clocks. These clocks influence when an organ operates most efficiently:

- **Daytime**: the pancreas is most responsive in secreting insulin, and muscles have the greatest capacity to take up glucose—well suited to eating and digestion;
- **Nighttime**: the body shifts toward repair—growth hormone release, cellular autophagy, and immune housekeeping.

Eating before sleep is like forcing the digestive system to work overtime after its shift while interrupting repair processes. This is called **circadian misalignment**.

> Key reference: Panda S. [Fasting, Circadian Rhythms, and Time-Restricted Feeding in Healthy Lifespan](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27304506/). Cell Metabolism, 2016.

## 2. Insulin Sensitivity: The Same Meal Causes a Higher Glucose Response at Night

The responsiveness of pancreatic β cells to glucose and the insulin sensitivity of muscle both follow circadian rhythms—**highest in the morning and lowest at night**.

More importantly, **melatonin, the sleep hormone, begins to rise in the evening, and melatonin directly inhibits pancreatic insulin secretion**. This means that when someone eats at night, the body's ability to manage blood glucose is at its weakest.

The glucose response to the same 500 kcal differs substantially when eaten at 8 a.m. versus 10 p.m.

> Key reference: [A Time to Rest, a Time to Dine: Sleep, Time-Restricted Eating, and Cardiometabolic Health](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35276787/). Nutrients, 2022.

## 3. Growth Hormone: Eating Before Sleep Can Suppress It

About **70% of human growth hormone (GH) is released during deep, slow-wave sleep**, concentrated in the first half of the night after falling asleep. GH contributes to:

- Muscle repair and growth
- Fat breakdown
- Cellular regeneration

**Insulin directly inhibits GH**. Eating before bed, especially carbohydrates, raises blood glucose, triggers insulin secretion, and can suppress GH release. This weakens one of the day's most important repair windows.

> Key reference: [Eat, Train, Sleep-Retreat? Hormonal Interactions of Intermittent Fasting, Exercise and Circadian Rhythm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33808424/). Biomolecules, 2021.

## 4. Autophagy and the mTOR/AMPK Switch: The Body Needs "Building" and "Cleaning" Shifts

Cells have two central pathways:

| Pathway | When It Is Active | What It Does |
|---|---|---|
| **mTOR** (building pathway) | Activated after eating | Synthesizes protein and builds cells |
| **AMPK** (repair pathway) | Activated during fasting | Initiates autophagy, clears damaged organelles, and recycles waste |

The body needs **two alternating shifts**: building during the day and cleaning at night.

Eating before sleep keeps mTOR active, suppresses AMPK and autophagy, and leaves less time for clearing cellular "waste."

The 2016 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine was awarded to Yoshinori Ohsumi for discoveries of mechanisms for autophagy.

> Key reference: [Circadian rhythms, time-restricted feeding, and healthy aging](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28017879/). Ageing Research Reviews, 2017.

## 5. Sleep Quality: Digestion Raises Body Temperature

Falling asleep requires a **decline in core body temperature**. Digestion produces heat and raises core temperature, potentially causing:

- Slower sleep onset
- Less deep sleep
- More nighttime awakenings

## Summary: Five Layers Working Together

`
Avoiding food before sleep
  ├─ 1. Circadian alignment: organs do the right work at the right time
  ├─ 2. Insulin sensitivity: avoid eating when glucose handling is weakest
  ├─ 3. Growth hormone: protect the deep-sleep peak in GH release
  ├─ 4. Autophagy/mTOR: allow the body to complete its nighttime "cleaning" shift
  └─ 5. Sleep quality: avoid digestion-related heat interfering with sleep onset
`

These layers are not independent; they reinforce one another. This is why Bryan Johnson's $2 million medical team compresses the eating window into the morning. The point is not to eat less, but to **align eating time with the body's physiology**.

## Is Water Allowed?

**Yes.** Plain water has no calories, does not trigger insulin, does not affect mTOR/AMPK, and does not interfere with any mechanism above. A moderate amount before bed is fine; avoid enough to cause nocturia.

## Is Milk Allowed?

Strictly speaking, **milk counts as food**:

- One glass contains about 120 kcal, including protein (casein + whey), lactose (sugar), and fat;
- Protein and sugar both trigger insulin and mTOR;
- It shortens the fasting window and delays the onset of autophagy.

The practical effect depends on the amount:

- **A small sip (no more than 50 ml)**: minimal effect and not worth worrying about;
- **A full glass (200–250 ml)**: equivalent to a small snack and partially interrupts the mechanisms above;
- If the goal is to maximize the benefits of fasting before sleep, **not drinking milk is best**;
- If desired, choose **a small amount of warm milk (no more than 100 ml)**; the effect is limited.

# Vitamin D and Autoimmune Disease Update Log

Date: 2026-07-21

## Origin

A second-hand short-video script mentioned VITAL's 22%/39% figures, post-discontinuation follow-up, Treg mechanisms, family-history dosing, a 40 ng/mL target, and a D3+K2 combination. This triggered a claim-by-claim source review.

## Work in This Round

1. Checked the full text of the original 2022 VITAL paper and the full 2024 extended follow-up;
2. Recorded relative effects, absolute event counts, confidence intervals, prespecified lag analyses, and time interactions together;
3. Checked a Treg systematic review and a small type 1 diabetes RCT, correcting the between-group values in the script;
4. Used a 2025 meta-analysis of incident autoimmune disease as inconsistent evidence;
5. Used NIH ODS and the 2024 Endocrine Society guideline to correct claims about upper intake levels, thresholds, screening, and K2;
6. Updated the vitamin D3 dossier and completed an independent audit.

## Decisions

- Tier remains `T2`: the signal for a composite autoimmune outcome raises research value, while dosing for the general population and those with family history should be assessed by a clinician;
- Do not adopt "the golden window for immune tolerance" as a factual title;
- Do not adopt 4000–5000 IU/day, >40 ng/mL, or K2 90–200 μg as a project protocol;
- Added "How to interpret family history" and "How to read VITAL/post-discontinuation follow-up" to support quick judgments by general readers.

## Future Updates

- Reassess if randomized trials appear in autoantibody-positive or strong-family-history populations with clinical onset as the endpoint;
- Update the dossier if VITAL publishes preregistered long-term results by disease, baseline 25(OH)D, and BMI;
- Product quality requires separate dossiers for specific D3/K2 SKUs and is not replaced by this efficacy audit.

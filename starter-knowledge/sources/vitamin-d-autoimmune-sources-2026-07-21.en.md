# Vitamin D and Autoimmune Disease Source Record

## Search Scope

- Databases/institutions: PubMed, PMC, BMJ, NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, and the Endocrine Society;
- Search date: 2026-07-21;
- Core search concepts: `VITAL vitamin D autoimmune disease`, `postintervention follow-up`, `vitamin D regulatory T cells randomized systematic review`, and `incident autoimmune disease meta-analysis`;
- Inclusion priorities: randomized clinical-event trials and follow-up, systematic reviews, and official intake and safety boundaries;
- Excluded: retellings in short videos, commercial supplement pages, and articles deriving doses solely from animal or cell experiments.

## Review of Circulating Claims

| Claim | Assessment | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| 2000 IU/day for five years reduced risk by 22% | Broadly accurate | Refers to relative risk for the composite outcome; 123 vs 155 cases, HR 0.78, absolute difference approximately 0.25 percentage points, P=0.05 |
| 39% reduction over the final three years | Figure has a source | HR 0.61 after excluding the first two years; the relationship between effect and time remains exploratory |
| Protection disappeared two years after stopping | Directionally accurate | Cumulative HR 0.98 during the observational extension; affected by independent supplementation, with variable individual responses after stopping |
| 4000–5000 IU/day for people with a family history | No direct trial evidence | VITAL was not a dedicated high-risk family-history trial; the positive family-history interaction applied to Omega-3 |
| 25(OH)D must be >40 ng/mL | Not supported by official guidelines | No universal optimal threshold for disease prevention has been established; NIH ODS considers ≥20 ng/mL sufficient for most people |
| D3+K2 combination | K2 data continue to accumulate | Effects of K2 on arterial calcification/coronary disease require further evidence, and it has an important interaction with warfarin |
| People with obesity should automatically increase dose | Partially supported only for “raising blood levels” | Benefit was weaker at higher BMI in VITAL, and investigators note that adipose dilution is not the only explanation |
| Increased Treg proves establishment of immune tolerance | Overinterpretation | Small trials and reviews support a surrogate-marker signal that should be interpreted with clinical outcomes |

## In Plain Language: What Might It Do?

AKG is like a traffic hub in the cell’s energy- and amino-acid-metabolism networks. It participates in the tricarboxylic-acid cycle and can influence nitrogen metabolism, oxygen sensing, and some epigenetic enzymes. Researchers have therefore proposed that supplementing AKG might affect inflammation, metabolism, and aging-related regulation.

However, the fact that the body naturally contains a substance does not mean that taking more is beneficial. Participation in many pathways also does not mean that oral intake produces a net benefit in the right tissue at the right time. Ca-AKG provides calcium as well, and product form, release profile, and combination vitamins alter actual exposure.

## Why Did It Suddenly Attract Attention?

A 2020 mouse study reported that Ca-AKG extended median lifespan and compressed late-life morbidity. Published in *Cell Metabolism*, it provided the central rationale for advancing to human trials.

A subsequent 42-person analysis of the commercial combination `Rejuvant®` reported that DNA-methylation age fell by about eight years on average after approximately seven months of use. This provided an initial signal of improved human biological age from Ca-AKG, which is now being tested in the ABLE randomized controlled trial.

## How Far Has the Human Evidence Progressed?

### 1. Before-and-After Comparison of a Combination in 42 People

- Design: retrospective, uncontrolled;
- Intervention: a commercial combination of Ca-AKG and vitamins;
- Outcome: DNA-methylation clock;
- Report: mean biological age fell by about eight years;
- Key limitations: regression to the mean, natural variation, lifestyle changes, selection bias, attribution within a combination, and commercial interests cannot be excluded.

### 2. Cross-Sectional Cohort of 4,260 Health Enthusiasts

A 2026 cross-sectional analysis used a proprietary 9-CpG salivary clock and found that users of a slow-release Ca-AKG-and-vitamin product had age residuals about 1.8 years lower on average. The association among users of ordinary AKG was smaller and not statistically significant. In a longitudinal subset, results were no longer significant after multivariable adjustment.

Such studies identify an association between use and biological age and can guide the design of subsequent randomized trials.

### 3. Key Randomized Trials Are Underway

The ABLE trial plans to enroll 120 adults aged 40–60 whose methylation age exceeds their chronological age. It will compare slow-release Ca-AKG at 1 g/day with placebo for six months; the primary outcome remains change in DNA-methylation age. A 2025 publication covered recruitment feasibility rather than efficacy; the efficacy results remain of interest.

Another registered 30-person trial uses Ca-AKG at 2 g/day for 12 weeks, with change in PhenoAge as the primary outcome. This dossier will be updated after results are published.

## How Should “Lower Biological Age” Be Understood?

DNA-methylation clocks are among the most important surrogate endpoints in aging research, with relevant methods published in leading journals such as *Nature* and *Cell*. Ca-AKG studies use multiple clocks for cross-validation and assess them alongside functional outcomes:

- Improved physical function;
- Changes in disease or mortality risk;
- Cross-validation across multiple clocks and laboratories.

Open Longevity classifies methylation clocks as surrogate endpoints and combines them with functional outcomes when assessing the longevity value of Ca-AKG.

## Food, AKG, and Ca-AKG

AKG is a human metabolite and is related to protein and amino-acid metabolism; ordinary food does not provide it in a defined “Ca-AKG dose.” Ordinary AKG, Ca-AKG, slow-release Ca-AKG, and vitamin-containing combination products are not the same intervention.

Product labels should record at least:

- Whether the product contains AKG or Ca-AKG;
- Ca-AKG and elemental-calcium content per serving;
- Whether it is slow-release;
- Whether it contains vitamins A or D or other active ingredients;
- Batch purity, contaminants, and independent testing.

## If You Still Choose Personal Exploration

Bryan Johnson’s protocol previously included it. Registered trials use 1–2 g/day.

Minimum recording requirements:

1. First record total calcium intake, kidney-stone history, kidney function, and any calcium/vitamin D products in use;
2. Use a single-ingredient product to avoid attributing a combination result to Ca-AKG;
3. Change only one candidate at a time;
4. Do not use a single consumer “biological age” report as the success criterion;
5. Prespecify duration, functional goals, and stopping conditions;
6. Do not self-experiment with hypercalcemia, kidney stones, severe kidney disease, pregnancy or breastfeeding, or complex medication use.

## Safety Boundaries

Ca-AKG increases calcium exposure, so total calcium intake, kidney stones, and disorders of calcium metabolism must be considered. Commercial combinations may also contain fat-soluble vitamins and must be fully recorded. People with hypercalcemia, kidney stones, severe kidney disease, during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or taking multiple medicines should first be assessed by a physician.

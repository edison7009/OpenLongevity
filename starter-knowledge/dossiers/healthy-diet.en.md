# Healthy Diet

::: tip 30-second summary
A healthy diet is a T1 cornerstone of Open Longevity. The goal is not to pursue a single definitive menu, but to establish an overall dietary pattern that is sustainable, nutritionally adequate, and appropriate for the individual’s health.
:::

## Core Principles

- Build the diet around vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes, nuts, and other nutrient-dense foods;
- Obtain adequate protein and total energy for age, activity level, and health status;
- Prefer unsaturated fats, while reducing trans fats, sugar-sweetened beverages, and highly processed foods;
- Continue adjusting the plan in light of blood pressure, glucose, blood lipids, weight change, and actual adherence.

## Why There Is No Single Prescribed Diet

Healthy dietary patterns such as the Mediterranean diet, DASH, and AHEI differ in their specific forms but share many fundamental principles. The product records these transferable common principles and then shapes a specific plan around the individual’s food culture, disease risk, and preferences.

## Boundaries in Older Age

Older adults should not restrict calories indiscriminately for weight loss or “anti-aging.” Unplanned weight loss, swallowing or chewing difficulties, kidney disease, or changes to diabetes medication should prompt assessment by a physician or registered dietitian.

# NAD+

::: tip 30-second summary
NMN and NR are two different NAD⁺ precursors. NAD⁺ metabolism has repeatedly been reported in leading journals such as *Science*, *Nature*, and *Cell* and is one of longevity research’s central pathways. Randomized human trials show that both can significantly raise blood NAD⁺ levels, and some studies report positive signals in walking speed, sleep, and metabolic measures. **Included in Bryan Johnson’s protocol: NR 450 mg or NMN 500 mg.**
:::

## In Plain Language: What Do They Do in the Body?

NAD⁺ is like a shared “work credential” for many intracellular reactions. With aging, NAD⁺ levels change in some tissues and models, suggesting an intuitive hypothesis: providing more precursor might help cells work efficiently again.

NMN and NR both enter NAD⁺ synthesis pathways but are not the same molecule. A rise in blood NAD⁺ measures after use shows that the precursor entered the pathway and was used by cells. NAD⁺ is foundational to energy metabolism, DNA repair, and many cellular reactions; its levels decline with age, and precursor supplementation is a direct way to restore NAD⁺.

This topic uses **NAD+** as the umbrella entry. NMN and NR are commonly discussed precursors, NADH is the reduced form of NAD⁺, and oral or intravenous NAD⁺ represents yet another delivery route. Similar names do not make them the same intervention.

## How Do NMN and NR Differ?

| Item | NMN | NR |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | Nicotinamide mononucleotide | Nicotinamide riboside |

| Current human signals | Higher NAD-related measures; positive secondary outcomes in walking, sleep, or metabolism | Higher NAD-related measures; signals in small trials of some diseases |

## How Far Has the Human Evidence Progressed?

### 1. Progress in Muscle and Physical-Function Research

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis included randomized trials with mean ages of 60.9–83 years. Pooled effects of NMN on skeletal-muscle index, grip strength, and walking speed were not significant, which may relate to the short intervention periods and small samples in included trials. Individual measures in specific populations, such as people with peripheral artery disease, showed positive NR signals. Longer, larger functional-outcome trials are underway.

### 2. NMN: Positive Signals Across Several Outcomes

A 2024 randomized double-blind trial of 60 participants used NMN at 250 mg/day for 12 weeks and reported positive signals in four-meter walking time, blood NAD metabolites, and some subjective sleep measures. It provides human evidence for possible functional benefit from NMN.

### 3. NR: Significant NAD⁺ Increase and Good Tolerability

A randomized trial in 20 older adults with mild cognitive impairment titrated NR to 1 g/day for 10 weeks. Blood NAD⁺ rose to about 2.6 times baseline, and adverse-event reports did not differ between groups, supporting NAD⁺ elevation and good tolerability in older adults. Cerebral blood flow and epigenetic clocks were exploratory signals.

## Food and Supplements

Food can provide sources of vitamin B3 such as nicotinamide, and small amounts of NMN/NR may occur in foods. An adequate normal dietary supply of B3 and high-dose use of an NAD⁺ precursor are separate questions.

Supplement labels should at minimum distinguish:

- NMN from NR, rather than stating only “NAD booster”;
- Actual content per serving, batch testing, storage, and expiration;
- Single-ingredient versus combination products;
- Whether the chemical form matches that used in research.

There is currently no established “effective longevity dose.” The 250–1000 mg/day used in research is an exposure range from trials, not individual dose advice.

## If You Still Choose Personal Exploration

This is not a recommended protocol, but a minimum recording standard to reduce confusion in self-experimentation:

1. Change only NMN or NR at one time; do not simultaneously add spermidine, PQQ, or Ca-AKG;
2. State the goal first, such as walking, training recovery, sleep, or a physician-approved measure;
3. Prespecify duration and failure criteria; do not substitute “higher NAD” for improved function;
4. Stop and assess if persistent gastrointestinal discomfort, rash, marked sleep changes, or other new symptoms occur;
5. Do not self-experiment during cancer treatment, pregnancy or breastfeeding, severe liver or kidney disease, or complex medication use.

## Safety Boundaries

Short-term trials report good tolerability. People receiving cancer treatment, during pregnancy or breastfeeding, with severe liver or kidney disease, or taking multiple medicines should first be assessed by a physician.

## Tier Assessment

- Current Tier: **T4**;
- Rationale: the NAD⁺ pathway mechanism is clear and published in *Science*, *Nature*, and *Cell*; human trials show increased NAD⁺; Bryan Johnson’s protocol includes it;
- Boundary: current human trials are mainly short-term, while data on long-term functional outcomes continue to accumulate;
- Evidence that would change the assessment: results from long-term functional or clinical-outcome trials such as NCT04691986.

## Ongoing Trial

`NCT04691986` is studying the effects of NR on VO₂max, muscle strength, and walking speed in 74 older veterans. Its status in the local 2026-07-20 snapshot is `ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING`; results have not yet been posted to the registry.

## NAD+ IV Clinics in Japan (2025–2026)

Japan, especially Tokyo, has recently seen many private-pay clinics offering intravenous NAD+ to affluent clients and medical tourists.

### Why Are They Especially Common in Japan?

1. **Private-pay medicine:** Anti-aging services are not covered by Japan’s health insurance, but legal private-pay care allows clinics to set prices and market services;
2. **Keio University association:** Keio University is a major center of Japanese NMN/NAD+ research, and many clinics cite “Harvard + Keio” directly in marketing;
3. **Anti-Aging Medicine Society ecosystem:** Japan has a mature anti-aging industry spanning screening, infusions, and supplements;
4. **Affluent clientele:** Clinics in Ginza and Omotesando serve affluent residents and medical tourists from China, Southeast Asia, and the Middle East.

### Relationship to the Evidence in This Dossier

::: warning Keep These Distinctions Clear
- These clinics’ NAD+ infusions are **not approved by Japan’s PMDA for anti-aging** and are provided as private-pay care;
- Prices are generally ¥30,000–¥100,000+ (approximately RMB ¥1,500–5,000) per session;
- The Harvard and Keio research cited in marketing mainly concerns animal and small human trials of **oral NMN/NR**, not IV infusion;
- IV NAD+ is a medical route and is reviewed separately from oral NMN/NR;
- Human-trial records are more complete for oral NMN/NR;
- “Many clinics offer it” does not mean “it works”; the business model is driven by market demand and regulatory room.
:::

### If You Are Considering Use

- Prefer a clinic with clear ingredient labeling, batch records, and an in-person physician consultation;
- Do not decide solely on marketing terms such as “Harvard” or “Keio”;
- Oral NMN/NR, for which product-level evidence exists, is a more transparent, less expensive, and more controllable option;
- IV infusion carries greater infection and allergic-reaction risk, and its dose cannot be reversed after administration;
- People receiving cancer treatment should not independently use NAD+ supplementation in any form.

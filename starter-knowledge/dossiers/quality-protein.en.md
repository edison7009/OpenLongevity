# High-Quality Protein

::: tip 30-second summary
High-quality protein is a T1 dietary cornerstone of Open Longevity: first meet individual requirements, then consider essential amino acids, digestion and absorption, overall food quality, and distribution across the day. Together with strength training, it supports muscle maintenance, but it cannot replace training.
:::

| Quick assessment | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Why T1 | A foundational nutritional condition for muscle and physical function that can be sustained through everyday food |
| Preferred sources | Fish, eggs, dairy, lean meat, soy, legumes, and appropriately combined plant proteins |
| How to track it | Total intake, distribution across meals, weight change, strength, and individual context such as kidney function |
| What to combine it with | Strength training, adequate total energy, and recovery |

## “High Quality” Does Not Mean Protein Powder Alone

In the product, “high-quality protein” considers all of the following:

- Whether total individual requirements are met;
- Whether it provides enough essential amino acids;
- Whether the food also provides valuable micronutrients;
- Whether it can be digested, absorbed, and sustained over time;
- How plant and animal sources are combined;
- Whether it matches strength training and total energy intake.

Older adults may need to pay greater attention than younger adults to protein adequacy and distribution. The ESPEN practical guideline on nutrition in older people provides a guiding intake of at least about 1 g/kg/day and emphasizes individualization according to nutritional status, activity, illness, and tolerance. This is guideline context, not a fixed prescription for every user.

## Safety Boundaries

People with chronic kidney disease, severe liver disease, certain metabolic diseases, or a prescribed restrictive diet should not independently adopt a high-protein plan. Protein powder is a convenience food, not a necessity for everyone, and it cannot replace a varied diet.

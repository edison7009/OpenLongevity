## Core Conclusion

Glutathione (GSH) is a tripeptide made from glutamate, cysteine, and glycine and is a central endogenous redox system. Small randomized human studies show that oral products can alter some GSH stores or absorption measures, but no trial has established longer life, fewer major diseases, or broadly improved healthspan.

## Four Approaches That Must Be Kept Separate

### 1. Direct Oral Reduced Glutathione

In a 2015 six-month double-blind randomized trial of 54 nonsmoking adults, both 250 mg/day and 1,000 mg/day increased GSH in some blood, lymphocyte, and buccal-cell compartments. This answers whether body levels can change, not whether supplementation extends life.

### 2. Liposomal, Micellar, Oral-Film, and Other Formulations

Special formulations may increase short-term exposure, but most evidence consists of very small pharmacokinetic or short-duration studies. Greater absorption does not automatically mean greater clinical benefit. Comparisons must use the exact formulation, dose, and finished product rather than the word “liposomal” alone.

### 3. NAC

N-acetylcysteine supplies cysteine for glutathione synthesis and is also a medicine with established clinical indications. Evidence for its medical uses cannot be transferred directly to chronic longevity supplementation in healthy people.

### 4. GlyNAC

GlyNAC combines glycine and NAC; it is not finished glutathione. A 16-week randomized trial in 24 older adults reported improvements across several biomarkers and functional measures. A separate two-week dose-ranging trial in 114 healthy older adults did not improve the overall primary GSH endpoint, with a signal only in a post hoc subgroup with high oxidative stress and low GSH. It should remain a distinct research candidate.

## How to Choose and Record a Product

- State the goal: a defined deficiency or disease context, an exploratory trial, or a vague “antioxidant” claim;
- Record the exact form: reduced, S-acetyl, liposomal, micellar, or another delivery system;
- Prefer a single-ingredient product with a clear dose and verifiable lot or independent testing;
- GSH inside a blend cannot be compared directly with single-ingredient trials or credited for the blend’s effects;
- Do not substitute “detox,” “skin brightening,” or “anti-aging” marketing for clinical outcomes.

## Safety Boundaries

Oral human studies generally report acceptable short-term tolerability, but long-term routine-use data are limited. People who are pregnant or breastfeeding, have asthma, liver or kidney disease, are receiving cancer treatment, or take complex medication regimens should discuss use with a physician. Intravenous and inhaled delivery are outside ordinary self-directed supplement use.

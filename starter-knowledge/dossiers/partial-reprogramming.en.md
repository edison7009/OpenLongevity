# Partial Cellular Reprogramming (Yamanaka Factors)

Partial cellular reprogramming attempts to activate reprogramming factors such as OCT4, SOX2, and KLF4 briefly, shifting some age-associated cellular states in a younger direction while avoiding the complete reset of mature cells into pluripotent stem cells. Its potential is substantial, but it remains a frontier biotechnology that must not be attempted independently.

| Quick assessment | Current answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | Temporary, controlled expression of reprogramming factors such as OSK or OSKM |
| How it differs from iPSC | iPSC involves complete reprogramming; partial reprogramming aims to preserve the cell’s original identity |
| Current progress | Animal studies report signals of tissue repair and lifespan extension; human research has only just entered localized, early safety exploration |
| Main risks | Loss of cell identity, abnormal proliferation, tumor risk, and challenges in delivery and dose control |
| Current tier | **T5: Frontier exploration** |

## What Did Bryan Johnson Do?

In 2026, Bryan Johnson publicly stated that cells derived from his blood had been reprogrammed with Yamanaka factors into induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), which he described as a “newborn version” of himself. Scientifically, this is not human cloning and did not directly make his body younger; it produced pluripotent cells in a culture dish with the same genetic background as him.

In theory, these cells can be further differentiated into neural, cardiac-muscle, retinal, or other cells for disease modeling, drug testing, and possible future autologous cell therapy. Whole-organ replacement and systemic rejuvenation remain distant possibilities, not established clinical outcomes.

## OSK Experiment in Aged Mice

A preprint study delivered inducible OSK to normal aged mice at 124 weeks of age. The study reported:

- Median remaining lifespan from the start of intervention was 8.86 weeks in controls;
- It was 18.5 weeks in the OSK group, a 109% increase in “remaining lifespan”;
- Median total lifespan from birth increased from about 133 weeks to 142.5 weeks; total lifespan did not double.

The experiment used AAV9 to deliver a gene-expression system. This was gene-therapy-style delivery, not CRISPR editing of the mouse genome. The results still require independent replication and larger-scale validation.

## Do Not Confuse It with Gene Editing for Progeria

Another study used a CRISPR-derived adenine base editor to repair the disease-causing `LMNA` mutation in mice with progeria, increasing median lifespan from 215 days to 510 days. This was etiological treatment for a severe monogenic disease and cannot be interpreted directly as a general lifespan-extension method for normal mice or humans.

## Why Is It Provisionally T5?

- Its biological potential may greatly exceed that of most supplements;
- Normal aging animals have already shown signals worth investigating further;
- Human efficacy, long-term safety, delivery methods, and reversible control remain unresolved;
- There is currently no actionable protocol for the general public, and unregulated cell- or gene-therapy providers should not be used.

# Soluble Dietary Fiber

::: tip 30-second summary
When soluble dietary fibers enter water, some form viscous, gel-like structures while others are more readily fermented by gut microorganisms. A major 2019 meta-analysis commissioned by WHO and published in *The Lancet*—185 prospective studies plus 58 clinical trials—found that higher dietary-fiber intake was associated with a 15–30% reduction in all-cause mortality, one of nutrition science’s strongest longevity findings. **Psyllium and oat/barley β-glucan have the best evidence for LDL reduction and bowel regularity; Bryan Johnson’s current protocol directly includes several fiber ingredients.**

## In Plain Language: What Does It Do in the Body?

Some viscous fibers can be thought of as water-absorbing gels in the gut. They swell with water, alter how food moves and nutrients are absorbed, and carry away some bile acids. The body then uses cholesterol to replace those bile acids, one explanation for lower LDL. Other fibers act more like food for gut microorganisms and produce short-chain fatty acids, but they also cause gas more readily.

## Which Foods Provide It?

- β-glucan in oats and barley;
- Legumes;
- Pectin in fruit such as apples, citrus, and pears;
- Vegetables such as okra and eggplant;
- Inulin-containing foods such as onions, garlic, and chicory.

Whole foods also provide insoluble fiber, micronutrients, and phytochemicals, so they cannot be assessed only by grams of soluble fiber.

## How Should Supplements and Labels Be Assessed?

- For LDL reduction or constipation, psyllium husk has more direct evidence than products labeled only “prebiotic fiber”;
- Look at the actual grams of fiber per serving and the specific ingredient, not only the total weight of a “fiber blend”;
- Begin with a lower amount, increase gradually, and take enough water to reduce bloating and swallowing risk;
- Effects of inulin, GOS, resistant dextrin, and polydextrose are not interchangeable.

## Editorial Conclusion

Soluble fiber is among the nutritional categories with the strongest longevity evidence. The 2019 WHO-commissioned meta-analysis in *The Lancet*, covering 185 prospective studies and 135 million person-years, found that higher dietary-fiber intake was associated with 15–30% lower all-cause mortality and lower risks of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and colorectal cancer. Psyllium and oat/barley β-glucan have the best evidence for LDL reduction and chronic constipation, and the FDA has authorized coronary-heart-disease health claims for β-glucan and psyllium. Choose the specific ingredient according to the objective. Bryan Johnson’s current protocol includes them directly.

## Research Question

- Population: evaluate high LDL, chronic constipation, abnormal glucose metabolism, and generally healthy populations separately;
- Intervention: specify psyllium, β-glucan, inulin, GOS, or another type, along with dose and physical properties;
- Comparator: placebo, dietary background, or standard treatment;
- Primary outcomes: LDL, ApoB, bowel response, HbA1c, and gastrointestinal adverse effects;
- Long-term cardiovascular events and lifespan cannot be replaced directly by short-term lipid changes.

## Potential Benefits

- Psyllium at about 10 g/day lowers LDL, non-HDL cholesterol, and ApoB in meta-analyses of randomized trials;
- Pooled analyses of multiple soluble fibers show an average LDL reduction, with substantial heterogeneity between ingredients;
- In chronic constipation, fiber supplementation improves treatment response and bowel frequency, with relatively strong evidence for psyllium;
- Some glucose measures may improve among people with abnormal glucose metabolism, but effects depend on baseline status, dose, and study quality.

## Practical Points

- Ingredients such as psyllium, β-glucan, inulin, and GOS differ in viscosity and fermentability; choose according to the objective;
- LDL reduction is one pathway to cardiovascular protection, while *The Lancet* cohort analysis directly assessed mortality outcomes;
- Changes in the gut microbiome and production of short-chain fatty acids are important mechanisms in fiber’s health effects;
- Begin with a lower amount, increase gradually, and take enough water;
- Use supplements to complement whole-food fiber from whole grains, legumes, fruit, and vegetables.

## Conditions of Use

- LDL reduction: prioritize viscous, gel-forming fiber, especially psyllium or β-glucan with standardized evidence;
- Chronic constipation: increase the dose gradually and ensure adequate water intake;
- If baseline dietary fiber is very low, increase slowly to reduce bloating;
- Record fiber in foods and isolated-fiber supplements separately.

## Safety Boundaries

- Common adverse effects are bloating, gas, and abdominal discomfort, especially after a sudden dose increase;
- Psyllium must be taken with sufficient fluid; people with swallowing difficulty, risk of bowel obstruction, or severe gastrointestinal symptoms should not use it independently;
- Fiber may affect absorption of some oral medicines; check product instructions or consult a pharmacist about timing;
- Products can differ in purity, swelling behavior, and actual fiber content.

## Evidence Summary

| Record ID | Study type | Population | Duration | Main result | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fiber-lipid-2023 | Systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of 181 RCTs | 14,505 adults | Varied | Mean LDL −8.28 mg/dL; approximately −5.57 mg/dL per additional 5 g/day | Extremely high I²; major differences in fiber types and populations |
| psyllium-lipid-2018 | Systematic review and meta-analysis of 28 RCTs | 1924 participants | ≥3 weeks | Median approximately 10.2 g/day; LDL −0.33 mmol/L and non-HDL −0.39 mmol/L | Mostly surrogate outcomes; long-term events unknown |
| fiber-constipation-2022 | Systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 RCTs | 1251 adults with chronic constipation | Mostly several weeks | Treatment response RR 1.48; >10 g/day, ≥4 weeks, and psyllium performed better | High heterogeneity; increased bloating |
| fda-fiber-chd | Regulatory evidence review | Specified food sources | Continuously updated | FDA permits coronary-heart-disease health claims for specified oat/barley β-glucan and psyllium sources | Applies only to specified sources and labeling conditions |

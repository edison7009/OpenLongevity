# DHA / EPA

::: tip 30-second summary
DHA and EPA are two long-chain omega-3 fatty acids. DHA is an important structural component of cell membranes in the brain and retina, while EPA is more often discussed in relation to inflammatory mediators and blood lipids. The REDUCE-IT randomized controlled trial published in *The New England Journal of Medicine* found that highly purified EPA reduced major cardiovascular events by 25%. Large cohort analyses associate higher fish intake and blood omega-3 levels with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality. **Included in Bryan Johnson’s current protocol.**
:::

## In Plain Language: What Do They Do in the Body?

Think of the cell membrane as the cell’s “outer shell and control panel.” DHA and EPA become part of this membrane and participate in the production of different signaling molecules. DHA is particularly abundant in the brain and retina; EPA/DHA can also reduce hepatic triglyceride output and regulate inflammatory mediators. REDUCE-IT established a reduction in cardiovascular events with prescription-grade, highly purified EPA.

## Which Foods Provide Them?

- Oily fish such as salmon, sardines, mackerel, trout, and herring;
- Shellfish can also provide some;
- Algae are among the original sources of DHA/EPA, and algal oil can be an option for people who do not eat fish;
- Flaxseed, chia seeds, and walnuts mainly provide ALA. Conversion to EPA/DHA in humans is limited, so these sources cannot simply be substituted at equivalent doses.

## How Should Supplements and Labels Be Assessed?

- Look at the actual **EPA + DHA** content per serving, not the total weight such as “fish oil 1000 mg”;
- EPA-only, combined EPA+DHA, and algal-oil products do not share identical evidence;
- Treatment doses for high triglycerides and general wellness doses are separate questions;
- Check independent quality testing, signs of oxidation, and storage conditions;
- At high doses, atrial-fibrillation and bleeding risks require assessment in the context of personal medical history and medicines.

## Editorial Conclusion

Omega-3 is one of the most extensively studied supplement categories. REDUCE-IT in *The New England Journal of Medicine* found that prescription-grade EPA reduced major cardiovascular events by 25%. Large cohort analyses associate fish intake and blood omega-3 levels with lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality, and the FDA has approved prescription-grade EPA for a cardiovascular indication. Selection depends on population and objective: prescription-grade EPA for people with high triglycerides and high cardiovascular risk, and DHA for brain and retinal support. Bryan Johnson’s current protocol includes it.

## Conditions of Use

- First assess weekly fish intake;
- Separate primary prevention in the general population, established coronary heart disease, and high triglycerides;
- Evidence for EPA alone cannot be merged with evidence for EPA+DHA combinations;
- General wellness fish oil cannot replace prescription products.

## Safety Boundaries

At high doses, monitor signals related to atrial fibrillation and bleeding. People with atrial fibrillation, those using anticoagulants, people preparing for surgery, or anyone at risk of bleeding should consult a physician first.

# Aerobic Exercise

::: tip 30-second summary
Aerobic exercise is a T1 cornerstone of Open Longevity. It trains the cardiorespiratory system, work capacity, and recovery, and is consistently associated with lower risks of all-cause mortality, cardiovascular disease, and multiple chronic diseases.
:::

| Quick assessment | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Why T1 | Consistent guidelines, extensive human data, and the ability to start at a low dose and progress sustainably |
| Primary outcomes | Cardiorespiratory fitness, metabolic health, endurance, and capacity for daily activities |
| How to track it | Weekly duration, intensity, heart rate, pace, power, and perceived exertion |
| What to combine it with | Strength training, daily walking, sleep, and recovery |

## Preserve Both Types of Capacity

Continuous low- to moderate-intensity training helps build an aerobic base; higher-intensity training is used to raise the upper limit of cardiorespiratory fitness. The product should record these separately instead of counting only a vague total of “exercise minutes.”

WHO guidelines recommend that adults perform regular moderate- and vigorous-intensity aerobic activity while also scheduling muscle-strengthening exercise. Large prospective studies show that moving from complete inactivity into the guideline-recommended range generally corresponds to the clearest difference in risk.

## What Should a Program Record?

- Type of activity and total weekly duration;
- Ability to sustain a conversation, perceived exertion, and heart-rate zone;
- Pace, power, or completion time on a fixed route;
- Resting heart rate, recovery speed, and cardiorespiratory-fitness trends;
- Pain, fatigue, and coordination with strength training.

## Safety Boundaries

People who have been inactive for a long time, have cardiopulmonary symptoms, or intend to begin high-intensity interval training should first assess their baseline capacity and increase training gradually. Stop exercising and seek medical attention immediately if chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, fainting, or neurological symptoms occur.

# Vitamin C

::: tip 30-second summary
Vitamin C is a classic antioxidant. In 2026, Guang-Hui Liu’s team at the Chinese Academy of Sciences published a primate study in *Cell Metabolism*: after middle-aged and older monkeys received vitamin C for about 40 months, biological age was significantly reversed across multiple organs—brain by 7.4 years, muscle by 4.4 years, and skin by 4.2 years. The proposed mechanism was direct inhibition of ACSL4, interrupting a “ferro-aging” pathway. Bryan Johnson’s protocol contains vitamin C. **Ordinary intake can be met through fruit, lemon water, and other foods; an additional supplement is not needed.**

## In Plain Language: What Does It Do in the Body?

Iron gradually accumulates in cells with age. Iron itself is not inherently harmful, but excess iron can “rust,” catalyzing oxidative damage. An enzyme called ACSL4 becomes more active as iron accumulates and acts like an accelerator for lipid-peroxidation chain reactions, eventually pushing cells toward senescence.

Vitamin C is proposed to act in two ways: it directly blocks the active site of ACSL4, turning off the accelerator, and activates the Nrf2 antioxidant pathway, applying the brake at the same time. Together these actions interrupt the chain of iron deposition → lipid peroxidation → inflammation → aging.

This mechanism has been investigated across cells and primates and represents one of the more complete recent evidence chains for an antioxidant intervention.

## Why Are Longevity Communities Interested?

In April 2026, Guang-Hui Liu, Jing Qu, and Weiqi Zhang’s team at the Institute of Zoology, Chinese Academy of Sciences published a paper in *Cell Metabolism* proposing the concept of “ferro-aging” and testing vitamin C in primates. This is the highest-level animal evidence to date in this field: not mice, but monkeys treated for 40 months.

Vitamin C had previously been viewed in longevity circles as foundational but unexciting. This paper returned it to attention with a clear target, ACSL4; quantified effects using multi-organ aging clocks; and a species closer to humans.

## How Far Has the Scientific Evidence Progressed?

### Core Study: Vitamin C Intervention in Primates (*Cell Metabolism*, 2026)

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| Subjects | Middle-aged and older cynomolgus monkeys, approximately equivalent to humans aged 40–50 |
| Dose | 30 mg/kg/day in drinking water |
| Duration | Approximately 40 months |
| Main result | Reversal of biological age across multiple organs |

Magnitude by tissue or cell:

| Tissue/cell | Reversal of biological age |
|---|---|
| Hippocampal microglia | 7.39 years |
| Pancreatic β cells | 6.97 years |
| Brown adipose tissue | 4.44 years |
| Muscle | 4.41 years |
| Skin | 4.17 years |
| Kidney | 3.38 years |

Ferro-aging markers fell overall by 50%–70%.

### Chain of Mechanistic Validation

1. Iron accumulation + higher ACSL4 + lipid peroxidation were observed in aging human cells;
2. Iron deposition and ACSL4 upregulation were confirmed in tissues from older humans and monkeys;
3. ACSL4 overexpression caused senescence in young cells, while ACSL4 knockdown alleviated senescence, providing a causal test;
4. A high-iron diet in mice, 2000 mg Fe/kg, impaired cognition and movement and caused liver damage; liver-targeted CRISPR knockout of ACSL4 improved outcomes;
5. Screening 100 ferroptosis-related compounds identified vitamin C as active;
6. Molecular simulation and point-mutation experiments confirmed a direct vitamin C binding site on ACSL4.

### Ferro-Aging vs Ferroptosis

Ferro-aging is chronic: iron and oxidative damage accumulate slowly and tissue function gradually declines. Ferroptosis is acute: cells die rapidly. In this context, vitamin C is proposed as chronic protection, not emergency treatment.

## Which Foods Provide It?

Vitamin C is widespread in fresh fruit and vegetables. Rich sources include fresh jujube, 200+ mg/100g; kiwifruit, 60–90 mg/100g; bell peppers, 70–130 mg/100g; strawberries, 50–70 mg/100g; broccoli, 50–90 mg/100g; and citrus, 30–50 mg/100g.

Reaching the RNI of 100 mg/day through ordinary food is not difficult. Approaching the pharmacological dose in the monkey study, 30 mg/kg or roughly 1500–2000 mg/day in a human equivalent, is not realistic through food alone.

## Supplements: Are They Worthwhile, and How Should Labels Be Read?

Bryan Johnson’s public protocol contains vitamin C within a combination. People who meet their needs through fruit, vegetables, and other foods generally have no reason to add a high-dose single-ingredient supplement solely because of a longevity narrative.

Research-dose context only: 30 mg/kg/day in monkeys, which scales crudely to about 1800 mg/day for a 60 kg adult. This is a pharmacological dose and is not a dietary target.

## Who Needs Extra Caution?

::: warning Safety Boundaries
- History of kidney stones: high-dose vitamin C, >1000 mg/day, increases oxalate excretion; use caution with calcium oxalate stones
- Hemochromatosis/iron overload: vitamin C enhances iron absorption, so people with iron overload need dose control
- Kidney impairment: impaired oxalate handling requires a lower dose
- Drug interactions: warfarin, for which high-dose vitamin C may reduce anticoagulant effect, and theoretical interactions with statins whose clinical significance is uncertain
- Gastrointestinal effects: a large fasting dose can cause diarrhea and gastric acidity; taking it with food may help
- Upper limit: 2000 mg/day according to the Chinese Nutrition Society
:::

## What Factors May Alter the Result?

- Baseline vitamin C status: people with deficiency benefit more than those who are already sufficient;
- Iron burden: the more iron has accumulated, the more active the ACSL4 pathway may be and the larger the inhibitory effect of vitamin C could be;
- Age: iron accumulation accelerates in middle and older age, potentially creating an intervention window;
- Kidney function: affects oxalate metabolism and vitamin C clearance.

## Relationships with Medicines, Supplements, and Foundational Habits

- Iron supplements: vitamin C substantially increases non-heme iron absorption and can enhance iron treatment, but people with iron overload should avoid this;
- Vitamin E: may appear with vitamin C in combination products; calculate totals from the complete label;
- Nicotinamide: can be in the same tablet, with no adverse interaction identified;
- Omega-3: no known conflict;
- Diet: taking high-dose vitamin C with iron-rich red meat increases iron absorption; people with high iron burden should consider separating them.

## How Can Benefit, Failure, or a Reason to Stop Be Judged?

- The study used multi-omics aging clocks that individuals cannot reproduce directly;
- Observable proxy measures include skin condition, energy, gum health, and wound-healing speed;
- Ferritin: a high value, >300 ng/mL, suggests high iron burden and makes the anti-ferro-aging rationale more applicable;
- Stop immediately and seek medical attention if kidney-stone symptoms such as flank pain or blood in urine occur;
- Reduce the dose or stop if gastrointestinal discomfort persists for more than a week.

## Editorial Conclusion

In 2026, vitamin C gained a new primate-level research signal involving biological age across multiple organs and an ACSL4/ferro-aging mechanism. Bryan Johnson’s public protocol also contains vitamin C. Because food sources are abundant, whether and how much to supplement should depend on diet, deficiency risk, kidney function, and stone risk.

## Tier Assessment

- Current Tier: T2, based on hard primate data and a defined molecular target, with long-term human RCTs still needed;
- Rationale: a 40-month *Cell Metabolism* primate intervention, multi-organ age reversal, causal ACSL4 validation, and adoption by Bryan Johnson;
- Boundary: primate evidence is currently the highest level of animal research, while long-term human RCTs remain awaited;
- Evidence that would change the assessment: a human RCT showing lower ferro-aging markers or improved biological age would raise it to T1.

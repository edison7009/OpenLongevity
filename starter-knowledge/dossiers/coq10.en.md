# Coenzyme Q10

::: tip 30-second summary
Coenzyme Q10 is an “electron carrier” in the mitochondrial energy-production chain. The human body synthesizes it, but levels fall significantly with age. The Q-SYMBIO randomized controlled trial published in *JACC: Heart Failure* found that CoQ10 supplementation in patients with heart failure reduced major adverse cardiovascular events by 43% and significantly lowered cardiovascular mortality; meta-analyses support improvements in endothelial function, blood pressure, and statin-associated muscle symptoms. **Included in Bryan Johnson’s protocol.**
:::

## In Plain Language: What Does It Do in the Body?

Mitochondria convert energy from food into ATP through a chain of electron-transfer steps. CoQ10 acts like a carrier that shuttles electrons along this production line. Under certain conditions, it also participates in antioxidant defense. Q10 levels decline significantly with age in the heart, liver, and kidneys, with an especially marked decline in the heart. Randomized trials such as Q-SYMBIO suggest that supplementation can provide cardiovascular benefits in high-need populations.

## Which Foods Provide It?

Meat, fish, nuts, and some plant oils contain small amounts of CoQ10, but endogenous synthesis is usually the primary source. There is no recognized “CoQ10-deficient diet” or recommended intake for healthy people.

## How Should Supplements and Labels Be Assessed?

- Common forms are ubiquinone and the reduced form, ubiquinol;
- Absorption varies greatly between formulations, so products cannot be compared by milligrams alone;
- Fat-soluble formulations are better absorbed when taken with food containing fat;
- CoQ10 may interact with medicines such as warfarin and should not replace standard treatment for heart failure or dyslipidemia.

## Editorial Conclusion

Human evidence for CoQ10 is concentrated in high-need settings. The Q-SYMBIO randomized controlled trial found that major adverse cardiovascular events in patients with heart failure fell by 43% and cardiovascular mortality declined; meta-analyses support improvements in vascular endothelium, blood pressure, and statin-associated muscle symptoms. Levels decline with age, making middle-aged and older adults and statin users populations of particular interest. Bryan Johnson’s protocol includes it.

## Conditions of Use

- Priority populations: patients with heart failure, statin users, and middle-aged and older adults;
- Record heart failure, statin-associated symptoms, migraine, and other contexts separately;
- Choose a formulation with reliable absorption, such as ubiquinol or ubiquinone taken with food containing fat.

## Safety Boundaries

CoQ10 may interact with medicines such as warfarin. It cannot replace standard treatment for heart failure, dyslipidemia, or other cardiovascular conditions.

# Spermidine

::: tip 30-second summary
Spermidine is a small molecule that occurs naturally in the human body and food. Its most compelling feature is its ability to activate an autophagy process resembling “cellular cleaning and recycling” in cell and animal experiments. Bryan Johnson’s protocol previously included spermidine. **Both food sources and supplements can be considered in ordinary use.**
:::

## Research questions for this round

- Population: generally healthy adults, older adults with subjective cognitive decline, and older adults with a specifically low immune response;
- Interventions: food sources, spermidine-containing extracts, or spermidine monotherapy at defined doses;
- Control: placebo or lower dietary intake;
- Primary focus: cognition, physical function, infection or vaccine response, adverse events and long-term clinical outcomes;
- Changes in autophagy markers, inflammation indicators or antibodies are not a proxy for healthy lifespan alone.

## In Plain Language: What Might It Do in the Body?

Cells make proteins and use mitochondria every day, but they also create damaged parts and structures that lose efficiency. The body has a recycling system called autophagy: it packages and disassembles cellular components that need to be eliminated, and then reuses some of the raw materials. The process of specifically cleaning up damaged mitochondria is often called mitophagy.

Spermidine affects this recycling system in cell and animal experiments, so it is often described as "helping cells take out the garbage." This metaphor is easy to understand, but two sentences must be added:

1. Autophagy is not better when it is stronger, but it needs to be in a proper dynamic balance;
2. Activating a certain pathway in a petri dish or mice does not mean that oral supplements can make people live longer.

## Why Might It Support Longevity?

The longevity story mainly consists of three layers of evidence:

1. **Mechanism layer**: Cell and animal studies have confirmed that spermidine promotes autophagy and affects pathways related to inflammation, cardiovascular and nervous systems. The relevant mechanisms have been published in top journals such as Science and Nature;
2. **Animal layer**: Improvements in lifespan or health indicators appear in multiple models;
3. **Population observation layer**: The European Bruneck cohort (published in the "American Journal of Clinical Nutrition" in 2018) followed 829 people for 20 years and confirmed that higher spermidine intake was associated with an approximately 40% reduction in the risk of all-cause death, and there was a dose-response relationship.

Together, these three layers of evidence support continued interest in spermidine’s longevity relevance. Bryan Johnson’s protocol previously included it.

## How Far Has the Evidence Progressed?

### The most important human trials

The 2022 SmartAge randomized, double-blind trial compared wheat germ extract containing 0.9 mg spermidine daily to placebo in 100 adults aged 60–90 years with subjective cognitive decline for 12 months.

The result is:

- The study completed 12 months of safety observation, and the adverse events in the two groups were roughly balanced;
- Exploratory analysis revealed some memory and inflammation indicator signals, providing reference for subsequent trial design.

This larger, longer trial provides human data on the long-term safety of spermidine.

Another 2024 trial assessed short-term tolerability in 37 healthy men aged 50–70 who received 40 mg of spermidine daily for 28 days. Bryan Johnson’s protocol previously included spermidine.

A 2026 double-blind randomized placebo-controlled pilot trial enrolled 40 adults over 65 years of age using spermidine 6 mg/day for 13 weeks after the third dose of SARS-CoV-2 vaccine. Studies have reported improvements in some memory B cells, antibodies and markers of immune senescence in people who were originally low responders to the vaccine. This is the first randomized trial to observe that spermidine improves human immune function signals, opening a window from mechanism to function:

- 40-person pilot study, specific population and time window;
- Primary outcomes were vaccine response and immune markers;
- The adverse events in the two groups were balanced and the safety was good.

## Which Foods Provide It?

Spermidine occurs naturally in a variety of foods, and the content will vary depending on the variety, maturity, fermentation, processing and detection methods, so it is not suitable to use a single milligram number on the Internet as an accurate prescription.

Relatively common sources include:

- **Wheat Germ and Whole Grain**;
- **Soybeans, beans and some fermented soy products**;
- **Mushrooms**;
- **Green beans, cauliflower, broccoli, spinach, green peppers and other vegetables**;
- **Nuts and seeds**;
- Partially mature cheese, but high-salt, high-saturated-fat foods should not be bulked up for spermidine.

::: info A more realistic way to eat
Rather than chasing a “spermidine superfood,” it makes more sense to incorporate whole grains, legumes, mushrooms, and a variety of vegetables into your long-term diet. The benefits of these foods do not depend on spermidine as a component.
:::

### How to Read Labels: Three Rules

1. **Look at the spermidine content, not the total weight of the extract**
   - Correct: "Contains 1 mg of spermidine per serving"
   - Nonsense: "Wheat germ extract 500 mg" (no idea how much spermidine is in it)

2. **Choose single ingredients, do not choose compound stacks**
   - The combination (spermidine + NMN + resveratrol + …) cannot tell which ingredient is working
   - Add new supplements one at a time and observe for 4–8 weeks

3. **See if there is any third-party testing**
   - Has NSF, USP, Informed Sport or Open Lot COA → Bonus points
   - Only manufacturer self-test report → Medium
   - Nothing → Do not select

## Who needs to be extra cautious?

::: warning Safety Boundaries
- Spermidine is involved in cell growth and metabolism. If you are undergoing tumor treatment or have complex diseases, you should first be evaluated by a doctor;
- Pregnancy, lactation and children should be used under professional guidance;
- Complex drug users should avoid adding multiple supplements on their own;
- Discontinue and evaluate if persistent gastrointestinal discomfort, rash, or other new symptoms occur.
:::

## Editorial Conclusion

Spermidine has an evidence chain extending from mechanism—autophagy research published in *Science* and *Nature*—through animal lifespan results and cohort analyses, including an approximately 40% lower risk of all-cause mortality in a European cohort, to a functional immune signal in a human randomized trial. Food sources include natto, wheat germ, mushrooms, and legumes; food and supplements can both be considered. Bryan Johnson’s protocol previously included it.

## Tier Assessment

- Current Tier: **T3**

- Applicable boundary: food sources are given priority, and supplements are based on test doses;

# PQQ

::: tip 30-second summary
PQQ is a redox-active small molecule that promotes mitochondrial biogenesis by activating the PGC-1α pathway, a mechanism reported in several leading journals. A 2022 randomized, double-blind controlled trial found that PQQ disodium salt at 21.5 mg/day for 12 weeks significantly improved multiple cognitive domains in middle-aged and older adults. **Bryan Johnson’s protocol previously included PQQ.**
:::

## In Plain Language: What Might It Do?

The body must create new mitochondria, maintain existing ones, and clear damaged mitochondria at the same time. PQQ activates the PGC-1α pathway to promote mitochondrial biogenesis and also has antioxidant activity, making it one of the few substances that may promote formation of new mitochondria. Because declining mitochondrial function is a core feature of aging, PQQ has become an important candidate in longevity protocols.

## How Far Has the Human Evidence Progressed?

### Single-Ingredient Cognition RCT: Improvements Across Multiple Cognitive Domains

A randomized, double-blind trial published in 2022 enrolled 64 healthy Japanese adults aged 40–79 who reported becoming more forgetful with age. Participants used PQQ disodium salt at 21.5 mg/day for 12 weeks; 58 completed the study.

The study reported improvements versus placebo in multiple Cognitrax cognitive domains, DECO, and MMSE-J, with no adverse events observed. Study context:

- It used the specific commercial ingredient `mnemoPQQ®`;
- The 12-week duration provides evidence of short-term cognitive improvement;
- Longer functional-outcome trials remain of interest.

This study provides randomized controlled evidence for cognitive benefit from PQQ.

### Combination Trial: Signals in Brain-Metabolism Measures

A 6-week 2024 trial in older adults with mild cognitive impairment used both molecular hydrogen and PQQ. Positive signals appeared in selected orientation and brain-metabolism measures. The combination design informs future single-ingredient validation.

## Food and Supplements

Trace amounts of PQQ can be detected in some fermented foods, tea, fruit, and vegetables, but estimates of food content and actual intake vary widely. PQQ is not an established essential nutrient and has no recommended dietary intake; there is no need to pursue a particular food solely to obtain PQQ.

Supplement studies may use PQQ alone, PQQ disodium salt, or combinations with CoQ10, hydrogen, or other ingredients. Labels should at minimum specify the chemical form, PQQ content per serving, batch testing, and other active ingredients. Single-ingredient research, combination research, and product marketing are not interchangeable.

The approximately 20 mg/day used in studies is only a common experimental exposure, not an established longevity dose or personal recommendation.

## If You Still Choose Personal Exploration

- Introduce only single-ingredient PQQ and do not begin it at the same time as NMN, spermidine, or Ca-AKG;
- Prespecify one observable objective and a fixed period;
- Do not substitute a subjective sense of “more energy” for blinded cognitive testing or clinical outcomes;
- If there is no reproducible functional change, stop according to a prespecified failure criterion;
- Do not self-experiment during cancer treatment, pregnancy or breastfeeding, severe liver or kidney disease, or complex medication use.

## Safety Boundaries

Short-term trials report good tolerability; the 2022 trial observed no adverse events. An FDA GRAS notice supports safety under specified uses and exposure conditions. People who are pregnant or breastfeeding, receiving cancer treatment, or taking multiple medicines should first be assessed by a physician.
